The Complete Guide to Opening a Pharmacy in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 10 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Pharmacies are essential businesses in every Nigerian community. With a population of over 200 million and a growing healthcare sector, the demand for quality pharmaceutical services continues to increase. If you're a registered pharmacist or planning to partner with one, opening a pharmacy can be a profitable and socially impactful business.

But pharmacies are heavily regulated in Nigeria. You can't just open a shop and start selling drugs. There are strict registering your pharmacyrequirements from the Pharmacists Council of Nigeria (PCN), NAFDAC, and other regulatory bodies. Let me walk you through the entire process.

Qualifications and Requirements

Who Can Own a Pharmacy?

In Nigeria, a community pharmacy must be owned or supervised by a registered pharmacist. The PCN requires that every pharmacy has a superintendent pharmacist who is responsible for the professional operations.

If you're not a pharmacist, you can still invest in a pharmacy, but you must partner with a registered pharmacist who will serve as the superintendent. The pharmacist must be registered with the PCN and in good standing.

Required Documents

  • Pharmacist's registration certificate from PCN
  • Annual practicing license (APL) from PCN
  • CAC registration of the business
  • Premises approval from PCN
  • Local government permit
  • Fire safety certificate

Choose a Location

The PCN has specific requirements for pharmacy locations:

  • Distance requirement: A new pharmacy must be at least 21 meters away from an existing pharmacy (though this varies by state and is sometimes waived in underserved areas).
  • Minimum size: The premises must be at least 10 square meters for the dispensing area, plus additional space for the consulting room, waiting area, and storage.
  • Accessibility: The pharmacy should be easily accessible to the public, preferably on a ground floor with good visibility.
  • Environment: The area should be clean, well-drained, and free from environmental hazards.

Popular locations for pharmacies include residential areas, near hospitals and clinics, commercial districts, and along busy roads. Rent for pharmacy space varies from ₦500,000 to ₦5 million+ per year depending on location.

PCN Premises Approval

This is the most critical step. Before you can open, the PCN must approve your premises. The process involves:

  1. Application: Submit an application to the PCN with details of the proposed pharmacy, including the location, layout, and superintendent pharmacist.
  2. Inspection: PCN inspectors will visit the premises to verify that it meets their standards.
  3. Requirements: The premises must have a dispensing area, consulting room, adequate shelving, refrigeration for temperature-sensitive drugs, proper ventilation, and a clean water source.
  4. Approval: If the premises meet all requirements, the PCN issues a premises approval certificate.

Set Up Your Pharmacy

Interior and Equipment

  • Shelving and display units: For organizing and displaying medications (₦300,000 - ₦800,000)
  • Refrigerator: For storing temperature-sensitive drugs (₦150,000 - ₦400,000)
  • Consulting room furniture: Desk, chairs, examination couch (₦200,000 - ₦500,000)
  • Computer and pharmacy management software: For inventory management and sales tracking (₦200,000 - ₦500,000)
  • Air conditioning: Essential for maintaining proper drug storage temperature (₦200,000 - ₦500,000)
  • Signage: Professional pharmacy sign with the PCN-approved logo (₦100,000 - ₦300,000)
  • Generator/inverter: For power backup (₦300,000 - ₦1 million)

Initial Inventory

Your initial stock should cover the most commonly needed medications:

  • Antibiotics and anti-infectives
  • Pain relievers and anti-inflammatories
  • Antimalarials
  • Vitamins and supplements
  • First aid supplies
  • Personal care products
  • Baby care products
  • Chronic disease medications (blood pressure, diabetes)

Initial inventory costs range from ₦2 million to ₦10 million+ depending on the size of your pharmacy and the range of products you stock.

Staffing

At minimum, you need:

  • Superintendent pharmacist: Must be present during operating hours
  • Pharmacy technicians: 1-2 to assist with dispensing and customer service
  • Sales assistants: For over-the-counter products and general customer service
  • Security/cleaner: Depending on your location and operating hours

NAFDAC Requirements

All drugs sold in Nigeria must be registered with NAFDAC. As a pharmacy, you must:

  • Only stock NAFDAC-approved products
  • Keep records of all drug purchases and sales
  • Store drugs according to NAFDAC guidelines
  • Report adverse drug reactions to NAFDAC

Startup Cost Estimate

For a standard community pharmacy in a Nigerian city:

  • Premises rent (1-2 years): ₦1 million - ₦5 million
  • Renovation and interior: ₦1 million - ₦3 million
  • Equipment and furniture: ₦1 million - ₦3 million
  • Initial inventory: ₦2 million - ₦10 million
  • Registration and licensing: ₦300,000 - ₦800,000
  • Marketing and signage: ₦200,000 - ₦500,000
  • Working capital (3 months): ₦1 million - ₦3 million
  • Total: ₦6.5 million - ₦25 million+

Revenue and Profitability

A well-run community pharmacy in Nigeria can generate monthly revenue of ₦1 million to ₦5 million+, with profit margins of 20-35%. Profitability depends on location, inventory management, pricing, and customer service.

Additional revenue streams include:

  • Health screening services (blood pressure, blood sugar)
  • Consultation fees
  • Delivery services
  • Selling personal care and beauty products

Final Thoughts

Opening a pharmacy in Nigeria requires significant capital, regulatory compliance, and professional expertise. But it's a business that serves a critical need in the community and can be highly profitable when managed well.

Focus on quality service, proper drug storage, knowledgeable staff, and competitive pricing. Build trust with your community, and your pharmacy will thrive.
