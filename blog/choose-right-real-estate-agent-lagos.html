How to Choose the Right Real Estate Agent in Lagos

By BusinessMen Editorial 6 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Lagos real estate is a minefield. Properties with disputed ownership. Agents who collect agency fees and disappear. Fake listings designed to extract "inspection fees" from desperate house hunters. If you're looking to buy, sell, or rent property in Lagos, the agent you choose can make the difference between a smooth transaction and a nightmare.

Here's how to find a real estate agent you can actually trust.

Why You Need a Good Agent in Lagos

Lagos is Nigeria's most active real estate market, and that means it attracts the most fraudsters too. A good agent does more than just show you properties. They:

  • Verify property ownership and documentation
  • Negotiate prices on your behalf
  • Handle the legal paperwork
  • Know the market prices in different areas
  • Protect you from scams and fraudulent listings

But a bad agent can do the opposite: show you properties they don't have authority to sell, inflate prices, collect fees from both sides, and vanish when problems arise.

Where to Find Real Estate Agents in Lagos

Nigerian Institution of Estate Surveyors and Valuers (NIESV)

The NIESV is the professional body for estate surveyors and valuers in Nigeria. Their Lagos branch can provide you with a list of registered and licensed practitioners. This is your safest starting point.

Business Directories

Platforms like BusinessMen list real estate agents and agencies with their contact details, service areas, and sometimes customer reviews. You can filter by location to find agents specializing in specific Lagos neighborhoods.

Referrals

Ask friends, colleagues, or family members who have recently bought or rented property in Lagos. A personal recommendation from someone you trust carries significant weight.

How to Vet a Real Estate Agent

1. Verify Their License

Every legitimate estate surveyor and valuer in Nigeria must be registered with the Estate Surveyors and Valuers Registration Board of Nigeria (ESVARBON). Ask for their registration number and verify it. An unlicensed agent is operating illegally.

2. Check Their Track Record

How long have they been in business? How many transactions have they completed? Do they specialize in a particular area of Lagos? An agent who knows Lekki inside out might not be the best choice if you're looking for property in Ikeja.

3. Ask for References

Request contact information for recent clients. Call them. Ask about their experience. Did the agent deliver what they promised? Were there any surprises? Would they use the agent again?

4. Understand Their Fee Structure

Standard agency fees in Lagos are typically 10% of the annual rent for rentals and 5% of the purchase price for sales. Some agents also charge a legal fee (usually 5%) and a caution fee (usually 10% for rentals). Make sure all fees are clearly stated upfront.

Be wary of agents who demand fees before showing you verifying the agent's credentialsproperties. Legitimate agents earn their commission when the deal closes.

5. Test Their Market Knowledge

A good agent should be able to tell you:

  • Current rental and sale prices in your target area
  • Upcoming developments that might affect property values
  • The history of specific properties they're showing you
  • Legal requirements for property transactions in Lagos

If they can't answer basic questions about the market, they're not the right agent for you.

6. Check Their Online Presence

A professional agent should have a credible online presence. Check their website, social media profiles, and listings on property platforms. Do their listings look professional? Are the photos real? Do they respond promptly to inquiries?

Red Flags to Avoid

  • Agents who ask for money before showing properties: This is the most common scam in Lagos real estate.
  • Unwillingness to provide documentation: A legitimate agent will show you the property owner's documents and their own authorization to sell or rent.
  • Pressure to pay immediately: Good agents understand that property decisions take time.
  • No physical office: While some agents work remotely, a proper office adds credibility.
  • Prices significantly below market: If a 3-bedroom flat in Ikoyi is listed at ₦500,000 per year, it's almost certainly a scam.
  • Refusal to let you verify documents independently: You should always be able to verify property documents at the Lands Registry.

Questions to Ask Before Hiring an Agent

  • Are you registered with ESVARBON?
  • How many years have you been practicing in Lagos?
  • Which areas of Lagos do you specialize in?
  • Can you provide references from recent clients?
  • What is your fee structure?
  • How do you verify property ownership?
  • What happens if there's a problem with the property after the transaction?

Final Thoughts

The Lagos real estate market rewards the careful and punishes the careless. Take your time to find an agent who is licensed, experienced, knowledgeable, and transparent. The right agent will save you money, stress, and potentially devastating legal problems.

And remember: if something feels off, trust your instincts. Walk away and find another agent. There are plenty of good ones out there.
