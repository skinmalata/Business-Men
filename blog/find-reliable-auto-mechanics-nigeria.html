How to Find Reliable Auto Mechanics in Your City

By BusinessMen Editorial 5 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Finding a good mechanic in Nigeria is like finding gold. You hear stories about mechanics who diagnose the wrong problem, replace parts that didn't need replacing, charge for work they didn't do, or keep your car for weeks when the job should take hours.

But there are good mechanics out there. Honest, skilled, reasonably priced mechanics who will verifying any mechanic before hiringtreat your car like their own. You just need to know how to find them.

Where to Start Your Search

Ask for Recommendations

The best mechanics are found through word of mouth. Ask your colleagues, neighbors, and friends. Pay special attention to people who drive the same make and model as you. A mechanic who's great with Toyota might not be the best choice for a Mercedes.

Check Online Directories

Platforms like BusinessMen list auto repair shops and mechanics with customer reviews, ratings, and contact information. You can search by your city and read what other car owners have experienced.

Visit Established Garages

Drive through industrial areas and commercial zones in your city. Look for garages that are organized, have proper signage, and seem busy with customers. A garage that's always empty probably has a reason.

How to Evaluate a Mechanic

1. Check Their Specialization

Some mechanics specialize in specific brands. If you drive a Honda, find someone who knows Hondas inside out. General mechanics can handle basic repairs, but complex issues require specialized knowledge.

Ask: "What brands do you work on most often?" A good mechanic will be honest about their strengths and limitations.

2. Observe the Workshop

When you visit a garage, look around:

  • Is it organized or chaotic?
  • Are tools properly stored?
  • Is there diagnostic equipment (computer scanners for modern cars)?
  • Are there other customers waiting or picking up their cars?
  • Does the mechanic explain problems clearly or just tell you what to pay?

A clean, organized workshop with modern equipment suggests a professional operation. A mechanic who can explain what's wrong with your car in terms you understand is someone who actually knows what they're doing.

3. Ask About Parts

Good mechanics use quality parts and will tell you whether they're using original, OEM (original equipment manufacturer), or aftermarket parts. They should also give you the option and explain the price difference.

Be wary of mechanics who insist on buying parts themselves without showing you the receipts. Some mark up parts significantly. Ask to see the parts before they're installed, and request to keep the old parts so you can verify they were actually replaced.

4. Get a Written Estimate

Before any work begins, ask for a written estimate that breaks down labor costs and parts costs separately. This protects you from surprise charges when you come to pick up your car.

If the mechanic refuses to give a written estimate or says "just bring the car, we'll see," take your business elsewhere.

5. Test with a Small Job First

Before trusting a new mechanic with a major repair, give them a small job first. An oil change, brake pad replacement, or battery check. See how they handle it: the quality of work, the pricing, the communication, and the turnaround time.

Red Flags to Avoid

  • Mechanics who diagnose problems without inspecting the car: A proper diagnosis requires looking at the vehicle.
  • Pressure to do unnecessary repairs: "Your engine needs a complete overhaul" for a simple oil leak is a classic upsell.
  • No warranty on work: A confident mechanic stands behind their work. Ask about warranty on both parts and labor.
  • Refusal to show you the old parts: If they won't let you see the parts they replaced, they might not have replaced anything.
  • Unusually low prices: If a quote is significantly lower than others, they might be using substandard parts or cutting corners.
  • Keeping your car for an unreasonable time: A job that should take a few hours shouldn't stretch into days without a clear explanation.

Building a Relationship with a Good Mechanic

Once you find a mechanic you trust, stick with them. A mechanic who knows your car's history can diagnose problems faster and more accurately. They'll also be more likely to give you honest advice and fair pricing because they value the ongoing relationship.

Treat your mechanic with respect. Pay on time. Don't haggle over every naira. A good mechanic is worth their weight in gold, and they know it. Fair compensation ensures they'll continue giving you their best work.

Final Thoughts

Finding a reliable mechanic takes effort, but the payoff is enormous. A good mechanic will save you money in the long run by doing the job right the first time, using quality parts, and giving you honest advice about what your car actually needs versus what it doesn't.

Take the time to find the right one. Your car (and your wallet) will thank you.
