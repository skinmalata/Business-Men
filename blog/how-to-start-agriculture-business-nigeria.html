How to Start an Agriculture Business in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 9 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Agriculture is Nigeria's oldest business and still one of the most profitable. With over 200 million people to feed, a growing middle class demanding quality food, and government policies supporting the sector, agribusiness offers real opportunities for entrepreneurs willing to put in the work.

But farming isn't what it used to be. Modern agriculture in Nigeria involves registering finding farming supplies and equipmentyour agriculture businesstechnology, proper planning, and business acumen. Let me show you how to get started.

Choose Your Agribusiness Niche

Agriculture is broad. You need to pick a specific area to focus on:

Crop Farming

Popular crops in Nigeria include cassava, maize, rice, yam, tomatoes, peppers, and vegetables. Cash crops like cocoa, oil palm, and rubber are also profitable but require more land and longer gestation periods.

Vegetable farming (especially tomatoes, peppers, and leafy greens) has a quick turnaround. You can harvest within 2-3 months and sell to local markets, restaurants, and food processors.

Poultry Farming

One of the most popular agribusiness ventures in Nigeria. You can focus on:

  • Broilers: Raised for meat. Ready for market in 6-8 weeks.
  • Layers: Raised for eggs. Start laying at 18-22 weeks and produce for about 12-14 months.
  • Hatchery: Breeding and selling day-old chicks.

A small-scale poultry farm with 500 broilers can generate ₦500,000 to ₦1 million in profit per cycle, depending on feed costs and market prices.

Fish Farming (Aquaculture)

Catfish and tilapia are the most common. Catfish farming is particularly popular because catfish grow fast and are in high demand. A well-managed pond of 1,000 catfish can yield significant returns within 4-6 months.

Livestock Farming

Goats, sheep, cattle, and pigs. Goat and sheep farming are particularly profitable around festive periods (Eid, Christmas) when demand and prices spike.

Processing and Value Addition

Instead of just growing crops, process them. Turn cassava into garri or starch. Process tomatoes into paste. Package and brand rice. Value-added products command higher prices and have longer shelf lives.

Secure Land

You need land suitable for your chosen agribusiness. Consider:

  • Soil quality: Test the soil to ensure it's suitable for your crops.
  • Water availability: Essential for irrigation, especially during the dry season.
  • Accessibility: Can you transport your produce to market?
  • Security: Farm theft is a real problem in Nigeria. Choose a location you can secure.
  • Proximity to markets: The closer you are to your buyers, the lower your transportation costs.

Land prices vary enormously. In rural areas of states like Benue, Niger, or Kaduna, you can lease farmland for ₦50,000 to ₦200,000 per hectare per year. In areas closer to cities, prices are significantly higher.

Get the Necessary Permits

Depending on your agribusiness type, you may need:

  • CAC registration: For your business name or company.
  • State Ministry of Agriculture registration: Some states require farmers to register.
  • NAFDAC approval: If you're processing and packaging food products.
  • Environmental permits: For large-scale operations, especially livestock and fish farming.

Source Quality Inputs

The quality of your inputs determines the quality of your output:

  • Seeds and seedlings: Buy from certified suppliers. Improved seed varieties from research institutes like IITA (International Institute of Tropical Agriculture) give better yields.
  • Feed: For livestock and fish, feed is your biggest cost (60-70% of total production cost). Source quality feed from reputable manufacturers or consider producing your own.
  • Fertilizers and pesticides: Use recommended types and quantities. Overuse damages soil and increases costs.
  • Equipment: Depending on your scale, you might need tractors, ploughs, irrigation systems, or processing equipment.

Plan Your Finances

Agriculture requires patience. Unlike a retail business where you buy and sell quickly, farming has production cycles that take weeks or months. You need enough capital to cover costs until your first harvest or sale.

Key costs to budget for:

  • Land acquisition or lease
  • Land preparation (clearing, ploughing)
  • Seeds, seedlings, or livestock
  • Feed, fertilizer, and pesticides
  • Labor
  • Equipment and infrastructure
  • Transportation to market
  • Working capital for the production cycle

Government programs like the Central Bank of Nigeria's Anchor Borrowers' Programme and Bank of Industry agricultural loans can provide financing at single-digit interest rates. Explore these options.

Find Your Market

Don't grow first and find buyers later. Know your market before you plant:

  • Local markets: Visit markets in your area to understand pricing and demand.
  • Restaurants and hotels: They need consistent supply of fresh produce.
  • Food processors: Companies that process raw agricultural products into finished goods.
  • Supermarkets: They buy in bulk and pay better prices than open markets.
  • Online platforms: Farm-to-consumer platforms are growing in Nigeria.

Manage Risks

Agriculture comes with risks. Plan for them:

  • Weather: Invest in irrigation to reduce dependence on rainfall.
  • Pests and diseases: Work with agricultural extension officers for prevention and treatment advice.
  • Price fluctuations: Diversify your products. Don't put all your money into one crop.
  • Theft: Invest in security, especially for high-value crops and livestock.
  • Insurance: Agricultural insurance is available through the Nigerian Agricultural Insurance Corporation (NAIC).

Final Thoughts

Agriculture in Nigeria is a business, not a hobby. Treat it like one. Keep records. Plan your finances. Understand your market. Invest in knowledge and technology. And be patient. Farming rewards those who stick with it.

With Nigeria's growing population and increasing demand for quality food, the opportunities in agribusiness are enormous. The question isn't whether agriculture is profitable. The question is whether you're willing to do it right.
