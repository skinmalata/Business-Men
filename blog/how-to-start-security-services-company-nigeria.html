How to Start a Security Services Company in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 8 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Security is a growing concern for businesses and households across Nigeria. From residential estates to corporate offices, banks to event venues, the demand for professional security services continues to rise. If you're looking to enter this industry, here's what you need to know.

Understand the Regulatory Framework

The security industry in Nigeria is regulated by the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) and the Private Guard Companies Act. Before you can operate, you need:

  • CAC registration: Register as a limited liability company.
  • NSCDC approval: Apply for a license to operate a private security company.
  • Police clearance: All directors and key personnel must have registering hiring and vetting security personnelyour security companypolice clearance certificates.
  • Minimum capital requirement: The NSCDC requires a minimum share capital (currently ₦20 million for private guard companies).

The licensing process can take 3-6 months, so start early.

Choose Your Security Services

The security industry offers multiple service types:

Manned Guarding

The most common service. Providing trained security guards for residential estates, offices, banks, and events. This requires recruiting, training, and managing a large workforce.

Electronic Security

Installation and monitoring of CCTV systems, alarm systems, access control, and electric fencing. This requires technical expertise but has higher profit margins.

Executive Protection

Close protection for high-net-worth individuals, politicians, and corporate executives. Requires highly trained personnel and commands premium fees.

Event Security

Providing security for concerts, weddings, conferences, and other events. This is project-based and can be lucrative during peak event seasons.

Consulting

Security risk assessments, vulnerability audits, and security system design for businesses. This requires expertise and experience but has low overhead costs.

Recruit and Train Your Team

Your security guards are your product. Hire carefully:

  • Conduct thorough background checks including police clearance
  • Require minimum educational qualifications (SSCE or equivalent)
  • Provide comprehensive training in security procedures, first aid, fire safety, and customer service
  • Ensure all guards are registered with the NSCDC
  • Provide proper uniforms and identification

Training is an ongoing investment. Send your supervisors to advanced training programs. Keep your team updated on the latest security techniques and technologies.

Get the Right Equipment

Depending on your services, you'll need:

  • Uniforms and ID cards: Professional-looking uniforms build client confidence
  • Communication equipment: Two-way radios for guard coordination
  • Security technology: CCTV cameras, alarm systems, access control systems
  • Patrol vehicles: For mobile patrol services
  • Office equipment: Computers, phones, and security management software

Pricing Your Services

Security services in Nigeria are typically priced per guard per month. Current rates range from:

  • Basic security guard: ₦60,000 - ₦120,000 per month per guard
  • Supervisor: ₦100,000 - ₦200,000 per month
  • Close protection officer: ₦200,000 - ₦500,000+ per month
  • Electronic security installation: Project-based, ranging from ₦500,000 to ₦10 million+

Your pricing should cover guard salaries, uniforms, training, equipment, insurance, and your profit margin. Don't underprice yourself. Cheap security is often ineffective security.

Get Clients

Start with your network. Tell friends, family, and colleagues about your services. Approach:

  • Residential estates: Estate associations are always looking for reliable security providers.
  • Corporate offices: Banks, oil companies, and large businesses need professional security.
  • Schools and hospitals: These facilities require security but are often underserved.
  • Event organizers: Partner with event planners to provide security for their events.
  • Government agencies: Through competitive bidding processes.

Startup Cost Estimate

For a small security services company:

  • Business registration and licensing: ₦500,000 - ₦2 million
  • Office setup: ₦500,000 - ₦2 million
  • Uniforms and equipment (for 20 guards): ₦1 million - ₦3 million
  • Training: ₦300,000 - ₦800,000
  • Marketing: ₦200,000 - ₦500,000
  • Working capital (3 months salaries): ₦3 million - ₦6 million
  • Total: ₦5.5 million - ₦14.3 million

Final Thoughts

The security services business in Nigeria is growing but competitive. Success comes from professionalism, reliability, and well-trained staff. Your reputation is everything in this industry. One incident of negligence can damage your brand permanently.

Invest in training. Pay your guards fairly. Treat them with respect. And deliver on your promises to clients. Do these things, and you'll build a security company that stands out in a crowded market.
