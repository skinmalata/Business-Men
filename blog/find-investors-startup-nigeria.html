How to Find Investors for Your Startup in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 8 min read Updated May 20, 2026

You've built a great product. You have customers. You're growing. But to scale, you need capital. Your savings are exhausted, the bank wants collateral you don't have, and your family has already contributed what they can. It's time to find investors.

Nigeria's startup ecosystem has attracted billions in investment over the past few years. Here's how to find them and win their support.

Types of Investors in Nigeria

Friends and Family

The most common first source of funding for Nigerian startups. It's accessible but comes with relationship risks. If the business fails, you might lose both money and relationships. Be transparent about the risks and treat it as a formal investment with clear terms.

Angel Investors

High-net-worth individuals who invest their own money in early-stage startups. In Nigeria, angel investors typically invest between ₦5 million and ₦50 million. They often provide mentorship alongside capital.

Where to find them: Lagos Angel Network, Nigerian Angel Network, and through referrals from other entrepreneurs who have received angel funding.

Venture Capital Firms

Professional investment firms that manage funds from institutional investors. Nigerian and Africa-focused VCs include:

  • Ventures Platform: Early-stage investments across sectors
  • Microtraction: Pre-seed and seed investments
  • Future Africa: Early-stage tech investments
  • TLcom Capital: Growth-stage investments
  • Partech Africa: Seed to Series B investments
  • Savannah Fund: Early-stage tech investments

VCs typically invest between $100,000 and $10 million+ and expect significant equity in return. They also expect rapid growth and a clear exit strategy.

Government Programs and Grants

Non-dilutive funding options:

  • Tony Elumelu Foundation: $5,000 seed capital for African entrepreneurs
  • Bank of Industry: Loans at single-digit interest rates for manufacturing and tech
  • NITDA: Grants and support for tech startups
  • CBN intervention funds: Various funding programs for specific sectors

Crowdfunding

Raising small amounts from many people through platforms. While still emerging in Nigeria, crowdfunding is growing as a viable option for consumer-focused startups.

Prepare Before You Pitch

Build a Solid Business Plan

Investors want to see that you've thought through every aspect of your business. Your business plan should cover market size, competitive landscape, revenue model, growth strategy, and financial projections.

Create a Pitch Deck

A 10-15 slide presentation that tells your story:

  1. Problem: What problem are you solving?
  2. Solution: How does your product solve it?
  3. Market: How big is the opportunity?
  4. Product: Show your product
  5. Traction: What have you achieved so far?
  6. Business model: How do you make money?
  7. Competition: Who else is doing this and why are you better?
  8. Team: Who's building this?
  9. Financials: Current revenue and projections
  10. Ask: How much are you raising and what for?

Know Your Numbers

Investors will ask about your metrics. Know them cold:

  • Monthly recurring revenue
  • Customer acquisition cost
  • Lifetime value of a customer
  • Monthly growth rate
  • Burn rate (how much you spend monthly)
  • Runway (how long your current cash will last)

Where to Find Investors

Startup Events and Competitions

Attend events like Disrupt Africa conferences, TechCabal events, and startup competitions. Investors attend these events specifically to find promising startups.

Tech Hubs and Incubators

Join hubs like CcHub, Ventures Platform, and When We Hub. These organizations have investor networks and regularly host pitch events.

LinkedIn and Twitter

Many Nigerian investors are active on LinkedIn and Twitter. Follow them, engage with their content, and build relationships before asking for investment.

Referrals

The best way to get in front of an investor is through a referral from someone they trust. Ask other founders, mentors, or industry contacts for introductions.

The Pitch Process

1. Initial Contact

Send a concise email or LinkedIn message with a brief description of your startup, your traction, and your funding ask. Attach your pitch deck. Keep it under 200 words.

2. First Meeting

This is usually a 30-minute meeting where you present your pitch deck and answer questions. Be prepared, be honest, and be confident.

3. Due Diligence

If the investor is interested, they'll conduct due diligence: reviewing your financials, talking to your customers, verifying your claims, and assessing your team.

4. Term Sheet

If due diligence goes well, the investor will present a term sheet outlining the investment amount, valuation, equity percentage, and key terms.

5. Legal Documentation

Lawyers draft the investment agreement. Have your own lawyer review it before signing.

Common Mistakes When Seeking Investment

Asking for too much or too little: Research what similar startups at your stage have raised. Ask for an amount that matches your stage and needs.

Not knowing your numbers: If you can't answer basic questions about your metrics, investors will assume you don't understand your business.

Focusing only on the product: Investors invest in teams and markets, not just products. Show them you have the right team to execute and a large enough market to generate returns.

Being dishonest: Investors will verify everything you say. If you exaggerate your numbers or hide problems, you'll lose credibility permanently.

Final Thoughts

Finding investors takes time and persistence. You might pitch 50 investors before one says yes. That's normal. Every "no" is an opportunity to improve your pitch and your business. Keep building, keep pitching, and keep learning. The right investor will come.

And remember: investment is not the only path to growth. Many successful Nigerian startups bootstrapped their way to profitability. Consider all your options before giving up equity in your company.
