The Importance of Business Insurance in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 5 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Why Nigerian Businesses Need Insurance

Nigeria presents unique risks that make insurance even more important here than in many other countries:

  • Fire outbreaks: Markets, warehouses, and commercial buildings are vulnerable, especially in areas with unreliable electricity and widespread generator use.
  • Flooding: Seasonal flooding affects businesses across the country, from Lagos to Port Harcourt to Kano.
  • Theft and burglary: Commercial areas are targets for organized theft.
  • Vehicle accidents: Nigeria's roads are among the most dangerous in Africa. If your business relies on vehicles, the risk is real.
  • Employee injuries: Workplace accidents happen, and you're legally responsible for your staff's safety.

Types of Business Insurance in Nigeria

Fire and Special Perils Insurance

This covers damage to your business property caused by fire, lightning, explosion, and other specified perils. It's one of the most common and most important policies for Nigerian businesses, especially those in markets or industrial areas.

The premium is typically 0.5% to 1.5% of the insured value annually. So if your shop and inventory are worth ₦10 million, expect to pay between ₦50,000 and ₦150,000 per year. Compare that to the cost of rebuilding from scratch.

Burglary and Theft Insurance

Covers loss of business property due to burglary, theft, or robbery. This is essential if you keep valuable inventory, equipment, or cash on your premises.

Goods in Transit Insurance

If your business moves goods from one location to another (and most do), this policy covers loss or damage during transportation. Given the state of Nigerian roads and the risk of accidents, this is particularly valuable.

Motor Insurance

Third-party motor insurance is mandatory by law in Nigeria. But for business vehicles, comprehensive insurance is worth the extra cost. It covers damage to your own vehicle as well as third-party liability.

Public Liability Insurance

If a customer slips and falls in your shop, or if your product causes harm to someone, public liability insurance covers the legal costs and compensation. This is especially important for restaurants, hotels, retail stores, and any business that interacts with the public.

Employers' Liability Insurance

Covers your legal liability for injuries or illnesses suffered by employees in the course of their work. Under Nigerian law, employers have a duty of care to their employees. This insurance protects you if that duty is breached.

Professional Indemnity Insurance

If you provide professional services (consulting, legal, accounting, engineering, medical), this covers you against claims of negligence, errors, or omissions in your work. A single mistake in professional advice can cost millions in damages.

Group Life Insurance

The Pension Reform Act requires employers to provide group life insurance for employees, covering at least three times the employee's annual total emolument. This isn't optional. It's a legal requirement.

How to Choose an Insurance Provider

Nigeria has over 50 licensed insurance companies. Here's how to pick the right one:

  • Check NAICOM licensing: The National Insurance Commission regulates all insurance companies in Nigeria. Verify that your chosen insurer is licensed on the NAICOM website.
  • Look at claim settlement history: An insurance company is only as good as its willingness to pay claims. Ask about their claim settlement ratio and turnaround time.
  • Compare premiums: Get quotes from at least three companies. But don't choose solely on price. A cheap policy that doesn't pay claims is worthless.
  • Read the policy wording: Understand exactly what's covered and what's excluded. Some policies have exclusions that can catch you off guard.
  • Use a licensed insurance broker: A good broker can help you find the right coverage at competitive rates. They earn their commission from the insurer, not from you.

How Much Should You Budget for Insurance?

For a small business, expect to spend between ₦100,000 and ₦500,000 annually on a basic insurance package covering fire, burglary, and public liability. Larger businesses with more assets, employees, and vehicles will naturally pay more.

Think of insurance premiums as a business expense, not an optional cost. Factor them into your annual budget the same way you factor in rent and salaries.

Common Insurance Mistakes

Underinsuring: Declaring a lower value for your property to save on premiums means you'll receive a lower payout when you make a claim. Always insure for the full replacement value.

Not reviewing your policy annually: As your business grows, your insurance needs change. Review your coverage every year and adjust accordingly.

Not disclosing all relevant information: If you fail to disclose material facts when applying for insurance, your claim can be rejected. Be honest and thorough.

Buying insurance only when required: Some businesses only get insurance when a client or landlord demands it. Don't wait for a crisis. Get covered before you need it.

Final Thoughts

Business insurance is one of those things you hope you never need but will be incredibly grateful for when you do. In Nigeria's business environment, where risks are real and recovery without insurance can be devastating, it's not a luxury. It's a necessity.

Talk to a licensed insurance broker today. Get quotes. Compare policies. And get covered before something happens that you can't afford to handle alone.
