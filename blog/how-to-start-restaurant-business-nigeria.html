How to Start a Restaurant Business in Nigeria: A Beginner's Guide

By BusinessMen Editorial 10 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Nigerians love to eat out. Whether it's a quick suya at the roadside, a proper jollof rice and grilled fish at a local bukka, or a fancy dinner at a restaurant in Victoria Island, the restaurant business in Nigeria is booming.

But here's the thing: restaurants also fail at an alarming rate. The difference between success and failure comes down to planning, execution, and understanding the Nigerian market.

Choose Your Restaurant Concept

Before you spend a single naira, decide what kind of restaurant you want to run:

Fast Food / Quick Service

Think Mr. Bigg's, Chicken Republic, or your local shawarma spot. Fast preparation, affordable prices, high volume. This model works well in busy commercial areas and near universities.

Full-Service Restaurant

Table service, broader menu, higher prices. This requires more investment in ambiance, staff, and kitchen equipment but can command higher margins.

Specialty Restaurant

Focus on a specific cuisine: Nigerian traditional, Chinese, Lebanese, Italian, or fusion. Specialty restaurants attract customers looking for something specific and can charge premium prices.

Cloud Kitchen / Delivery-Only

No dine-in space. You cook and deliver. This model has lower startup costs since you don't need a fancy location or dining area. Perfect for the growing food delivery market in Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt.

Buka / Local Eatery

Affordable, home-style Nigerian food. Lower margins but high volume. Works well in areas with lots of workers who need affordable lunch options.

Write a Business Plan

Your restaurant business plan should cover:

  • Concept and menu: What will you serve? Who is your target customer?
  • Location analysis: Where will you open? What's the foot traffic like?
  • Startup costs: Rent, equipment, renovation, licenses, initial inventory, staff salaries for the first 3 months.
  • Revenue projections: How many customers per day? Average spend per customer?
  • Marketing strategy: How will you attract customers?
  • Break-even analysis: When will you start making profit?

Find the Right Location

For a restaurant, location is everything. Consider:

  • Foot traffic: Is the area busy? Are there offices, schools, or residential estates nearby?
  • Visibility: Can people see your restaurant from the road?
  • Parking: Is there parking space for customers?
  • Competition: Are there other restaurants nearby? Competition isn't always bad, it can create a food destination.
  • Accessibility: Can delivery riders easily find and access your location?

Rent for restaurant space in Lagos varies wildly. A small shop in a suburban area might cost ₦500,000 to ₦2 million per year. A prime location in Lekki or Victoria Island could cost ₦5 million to ₦20 million+ per year.

Get the Necessary Permits and Licenses

Running a restaurant in Nigeria requires several permits:

  • CAC registration: Register your business name or company.
  • Local government permit: Food establishment permit from your local government.
  • NAFDAC registration: If you're packaging any food products, you need NAFDAC approval.
  • Fire safety certificate: From the state fire service.
  • Health inspection: Your premises will be inspected by health officials.
  • Liquor license: If you plan to serve alcohol.
  • Music license: From the Musical Copyright Society of Nigeria (MCSN) if you play music.

Design Your Menu

Your menu is your product. Get it right:

  • Keep it focused: Don't try to serve everything. A focused menu means better quality, less waste, and easier kitchen management.
  • Price strategically: Calculate your food cost (typically 30-35% of the selling price) and price accordingly.
  • Include signature dishes: Have 2-3 items that people will specifically come for.
  • Consider local tastes: Even if you're running a foreign cuisine, include options that appeal to Nigerian palates.
  • Test your recipes: Before opening, have friends, family, and strangers taste your food. Get honest feedback and adjust.

Equip Your Kitchen

Kitchen equipment is one of your biggest startup costs. You'll need:

  • Cooking equipment (cookers, ovens, fryers, grills)
  • Refrigeration (freezers, refrigerators, cold rooms)
  • Food preparation equipment (blenders, mixers, food processors)
  • Cookware and utensils
  • Serving equipment (plates, cutlery, glasses)
  • Cleaning supplies and dishwashing equipment

You can buy new or used. Used equipment from closed restaurants can save you 40-60% on costs. Just make sure everything is in good working condition.

Hire the Right Staff

Your kitchen and service staff will make or break your restaurant:

  • Head chef: The most important hire. This person sets the standard for food quality and kitchen operations.
  • Kitchen staff: Cooks, kitchen assistants, and dishwashers.
  • Service staff: Waiters, cashiers, and hosts.
  • Cleaners: Cleanliness is non-negotiable in a restaurant.
  • Security: Especially for evening operations.

Pay your staff fairly and on time. Restaurant staff turnover in Nigeria is high, and good staff are hard to find. Treat them well, and they'll stay.

Marketing Your Restaurant

Getting customers through the door is your first challenge:

  • Social media: Instagram is king for restaurants in Nigeria. Post mouth-watering photos of your food daily.
  • Food delivery platforms: List on Glovo, Chowdeck, or Uber Eats if available in your city.
  • Opening promotions: Discounts, free drinks, or combo deals to attract initial customers.
  • Google Business Profile: So people searching for "restaurants near me" can find you.
  • Word of mouth: Great food and service will bring customers back and bring their friends.

Startup Cost Estimate

For a small to medium restaurant in Lagos:

  • Rent (1-2 years): ₦2 million - ₦10 million
  • Renovation and interior: ₦2 million - ₦8 million
  • Kitchen equipment: ₦2 million - ₦6 million
  • Licenses and permits: ₦300,000 - ₦1 million
  • Initial inventory: ₦500,000 - ₦2 million
  • Marketing and launch: ₦500,000 - ₦2 million
  • Working capital (3 months): ₦3 million - ₦8 million
  • Total: ₦10 million - ₦37 million+

Final Thoughts

The restaurant business in Nigeria is competitive but rewarding. Success comes from great food, excellent service, consistent quality, and smart marketing. Don't rush into it. Plan carefully, budget realistically, and be prepared to work hard, especially in the first year.

And remember: in the restaurant business, your customers are your best marketers. Give them a reason to come back, and they'll bring their friends.
