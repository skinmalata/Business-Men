How to Verify a Business Before Making a Purchase in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 5 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Every day, Nigerians lose money to fake businesses. You've probably seen the stories: someone pays for goods online, the seller disappears. A contractor collects a deposit and never shows up. An "importer" takes your money for a car that doesn't exist.

The common thread in all these scams is that the victims checking if a vetting suppliers for your businessbusiness is registered with CACdidn't verify the business before sending money. Here's how to protect yourself.

Step 1: Check CAC Registration

The first thing to verify is whether the business is registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission. Every legitimate business in Nigeria should have an RC number (for companies) or BN number (for business names).

You can search the CAC public registry at cac.gov.ng to confirm the business exists, who owns it, and when it was registered. A business that claims to be registered but can't provide an RC number is lying.

Step 2: Verify Their Physical Address

Scam businesses often use fake addresses or PO boxes. If the business claims to have an office or shop, visit it. If you can't visit in person, ask someone you trust in that city to check.

Use Google Maps street view to look at the address. Does it match what the business claims? A business claiming to be a "major importer" operating from a residential apartment in a remote area should raise questions.

Step 3: Check Their Online Presence

A legitimate business should have some form of online presence:

  • Website: Does it look professional? Is it recently created? (You can check domain age on whois lookup tools.)
  • Social media: Are their social media accounts active? Do they have real followers and engagement, or are they mostly bots?
  • Business directories: Are they listed on platforms like BusinessMen? Verified listings with reviews add credibility.
  • Google Business Profile: Search for the business on Google Maps. Do reviews exist? Are they from real customers?

Be cautious of businesses that exist only on Instagram or WhatsApp with no other trace. While some legitimate small businesses operate this way, it's harder to verify them.

Step 4: Check Reviews and Testimonials

Search for the business name plus words like "review," "scam," "complaint," or "legit." Look at multiple platforms, not just the testimonials on their own website (which they can fabricate).

Check Twitter (X), Nairaland, and Facebook groups for discussions about the business. Nigerians are quick to call out scams on social media, and a problematic business will usually have complaints somewhere online.

Step 5: Verify Their Bank Account

When you're ready to pay, check that the bank account name matches the business name. If the business is registered as "ABC Enterprises Ltd" but asks you to pay into a personal account under a different name, that's a major red flag.

Legitimate businesses have corporate bank accounts. If they insist on personal accounts, ask why. Sometimes small businesses use personal accounts initially, but for significant purchases, this should concern you.

Step 6: Ask for References

If you're making a large purchase or entering a contract, ask the business for references from previous customers. Call those references. Ask about their experience, the quality of products or services, and whether they'd use the business again.

A legitimate business will be happy to provide references. A scammer will make excuses.

Step 7: Start Small

If you're unsure about a business, start with a small purchase before committing to a large one. Order a small batch of products. Pay for a small service first. See how they perform before trusting them with bigger money.

Red Flags That Signal a Scam

  • Prices that are too good to be true: If a brand-new iPhone is being sold for ₦100,000 when the market price is ₦800,000, it's a scam.
  • Pressure to pay immediately: "This offer expires today" is a classic pressure tactic.
  • Payment only through unusual methods: Cryptocurrency, gift cards, or money transfer services for business transactions are red flags.
  • No return or refund policy: Legitimate businesses have clear policies.
  • Poor communication: If they're slow to respond, evasive about questions, or get defensive when you ask for verification, walk away.
  • Generic email addresses: A "major corporation" using @yahoo.com or @gmail.com is suspicious.

What to Do If You've Been Scammed

If you've already sent money to a fraudulent business:

  1. Contact your bank immediately: They may be able to reverse the transaction if you act quickly.
  2. Report to the police: File a report at your nearest police station or through the EFCC whistleblower channels.
  3. Report to the CAC: If the business is using a fake RC number, report it to the CAC.
  4. Warn others: Share your experience on social media and review platforms to prevent others from falling victim.

Final Thoughts

Verification takes time, but it's time well spent. The 30 minutes you spend checking a business's credentials could save you hundreds of thousands of naira. In Nigeria's business environment, where scams are unfortunately common, being cautious isn't paranoia. It's good sense.

Always verify before you pay. Always.
