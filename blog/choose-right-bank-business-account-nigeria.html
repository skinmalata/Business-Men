How to Choose the Right Bank for Your Business Account

By BusinessMen Editorial 6 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Your choice of bank can affect your business more than you think. The wrong bank means failed transfers, terrible customer service, hidden fees eating into your profits, and spending hours in a branch queue when you should be running your business.

The right bank? It means smooth transactions, a reliable mobile app, dedicated account management, and getting your understanding your tax obligationsCAC certificate and TINfinancial products that actually help your business grow. Let me help you find the right one.

What to Look for in a Business Bank Account

1. Account Opening Requirements

Most Nigerian banks require similar documents for a corporate account: your CAC certificate, TIN, a board resolution (for companies), valid IDs of signatories, passport photographs, and a utility bill for your business address. Some banks make the process smoother than others. A few now allow you to start the process online and only visit the branch once for verification.

Ask about the minimum opening balance. Some banks require ₦50,000, setting up payroll for employeesothers ₦100,000 or more. For a small business, every naira counts.

2. Transaction Fees

This is where banks differ significantly. Look at:

  • Transfer fees: How much does each transfer cost? Some banks charge ₦50, others ₦100 or more per transfer. If you're making 20 transfers a day, that difference adds up to thousands of naira monthly.
  • POS transaction fees: If you accept card payments, what percentage does the bank charge?
  • Monthly maintenance fees: Some banks charge a flat monthly fee for business accounts. Others waive it if you maintain a minimum balance.
  • Cheque book fees: If you still use cheques (and many Nigerian businesses do), check the cost.
  • SMS alert fees: Usually ₦500 to ₦1,000 monthly. Some banks include it free for business accounts.

3. Digital Banking Capabilities

In 2026, your bank's mobile app and internet banking platform are just as important as their physical branches. Test the app before committing. Can you:

  • Make transfers quickly and reliably?
  • Generate account statements on demand?
  • Set up bulk payments for staff salaries?
  • Monitor transactions in real-time?
  • Set transaction limits and approvals for different staff members?

Some Nigerian banks have excellent digital platforms. Others still feel like they're stuck in 2015. Don't settle for bad technology when better options exist.

4. Customer Service

When a transfer fails at 4 PM on a Friday and you need to pay a supplier, you want a bank you can actually reach. Test their customer service before opening an account. Call their support line. Visit a branch. Send an email. See how they respond.

Ask other business owners about their experiences. Word of mouth is the most honest review you'll get. The business owner at the next shop knows more about which bank works than any advertisement.

5. Branch and ATM Network

If your business deals heavily in cash, you need a bank with branches and ATMs near your location. If you're in a commercial area like Balogun Market in Lagos or Ariaria Market in Aba, make sure your bank has a presence nearby.

That said, if your business is mostly digital (receiving payments online, paying suppliers via transfer), the branch network matters less. Focus on digital banking quality instead.

6. Business-Specific Products

The best business banks offer more than just a current account. Look for:

  • Business loans: Does the bank offer loans to small businesses? What are the interest rates? (Currently ranging from 15% to 30% depending on the bank and your credit profile.)
  • Overdraft facilities: Useful for managing cash flow gaps.
  • Fixed deposit accounts: For parking surplus funds at better interest rates.
  • Trade finance: If you import or export, you'll need letters of credit, form M processing, and other trade services.
  • Payroll services: Some banks offer automated payroll processing for staff salaries.

Top Banks for Small Businesses in Nigeria

While I won't rank them definitively (the best bank for you depends on your specific needs), here are banks that consistently get good reviews from small business owners:

GTBank: Known for excellent digital banking. Their internet banking platform is one of the best in Nigeria. Fast transfers, reliable app, good customer service. Their business account opening process is relatively straightforward.

Access Bank: After merging with Diamond Bank, they have one of the largest branch networks. Good for businesses that need physical branch access. Their digital platform has improved significantly.

First Bank: Nigeria's oldest bank with the widest branch network. If you're outside Lagos and Abuja, First Bank's reach is hard to beat. Their business banking products are comprehensive.

Zenith Bank: Strong corporate banking reputation. Good for businesses that plan to scale and need more sophisticated banking products. Their digital platform is solid.

Kuda Business: A digital-only option with zero maintenance fees and free transfers. Perfect for tech-savvy entrepreneurs who don't need physical branches. They offer business accounts with no minimum balance requirement.

Questions to Ask Before Opening an Account

  • What is the minimum opening balance?
  • What are the monthly maintenance fees?
  • How much do transfers cost (within the bank and to other banks)?
  • Is there a daily transfer limit, and can it be increased?
  • Do you offer a dedicated relationship manager for business accounts?
  • What loan products are available for small businesses?
  • How long does it take to open a business account?
  • Can I manage the account with multiple signatories?
  • What happens if my account is dormant?

Should You Open Accounts with Multiple Banks?

Many Nigerian business owners do, and there are good reasons for it:

  • Redundancy: If one bank's system goes down (and it happens), you have a backup.
  • Specialization: Use one bank for daily operations and another for savings or fixed deposits.
  • Loan access: Having a relationship with multiple banks gives you more options when you need financing.

But don't spread yourself too thin. Managing multiple accounts means more fees, more statements to reconcile, and more complexity. Start with one solid bank and add a second only when you have a clear reason.

Final Thoughts

Your bank is a business partner. Choose one that treats you like a valued customer, not just an account number. Visit branches, talk to account officers, compare fees, and test digital platforms before making your decision.

The time you spend choosing the right bank will save you countless hours of frustration down the road. And when your banking runs smoothly, you can focus on what really matters: growing your business and serving your customers.
