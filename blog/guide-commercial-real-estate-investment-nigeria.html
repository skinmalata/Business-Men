A Guide to Commercial Real Estate Investment in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 9 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Real estate has always been one of the safest investments in Nigeria. Land doesn't depreciate. Buildings generate rental income. And property values in growing cities tend to increase over time. But commercial real estate is a different game from residential.

Commercial properties (office spaces, retail shops, warehouses, hotels) typically generate higher returns than residential properties, but they also finding evaluating retail locationsthe right agentrequire more capital, more knowledge, and more active management. Let me walk you through what you need to know.

Types of Commercial Real Estate in Nigeria

Office Spaces

Office buildings in business districts like Victoria Island, Ikoyi, and Abuja's Central Business District command premium rents. A square meter of office space in VI can cost ₦30,000 to ₦80,000+ per year. The demand is driven by banks, oil companies, tech firms, and professional services.

Retail Properties

Shops in shopping malls, plazas, and commercial streets. Retail properties in high-traffic areas like Lekki Phase 1, Wuse Market, or Ikeja Computer Village generate strong rental income. Retail rents vary from ₦500,000 to ₦10 million+ per year depending on location and size.

Warehouses and Industrial Properties

With the growth of e-commerce and manufacturing, warehouses in areas like Apapa, Ikeja, and Ogun State industrial zones are in high demand. Warehouse rents range from ₦15,000 to ₦40,000 per square meter annually.

Hotels and Hospitality

Hotels in business and tourist destinations can generate significant income. But they require the most capital and active management. A mid-range hotel in Lagos or Abuja can cost ₦500 million to ₦2 billion+ to build.

Mixed-Use Developments

Buildings that combine retail on the ground floor, offices on upper floors, and sometimes residential units. These diversify your income streams and reduce vacancy risk.

How to Get Started

1. Define Your Investment Strategy

Are you looking for rental income, capital appreciation, or both? Rental income provides steady cash flow but requires active management. Capital appreciation is passive but you don't see returns until you sell.

Most successful commercial real estate investors in Nigeria aim for both: buy properties registering your real estate businessin growing areas where values will appreciate while collecting rental income in the meantime.

2. Research the Market

Understand the commercial real estate market in your target city:

  • What are the current rental rates?
  • What's the vacancy rate?
  • Which areas are growing?
  • What types of commercial properties are in demand?
  • What are the government's development plans for different areas?

Talk to estate surveyors, property developers, and other investors. Read market reports from firms like Broll Nigeria, Northcourt Real Estate, and EstateIntel.

3. Secure Financing

Commercial real estate requires significant capital. Options include:

  • Personal savings: The most common source for Nigerian investors.
  • Bank loans: Mortgage loans for commercial property typically require 30-40% equity contribution and charge 15-25% interest.
  • Joint ventures: Partner with other investors to pool resources.
  • Real estate investment trusts (REITs): For smaller investors, REITs allow you to invest in commercial real estate with less capital.

4. Due Diligence

Before buying any commercial property:

  • Verify the title: Check the Certificate of Occupancy (C of O) at the Lands Registry. Make sure there are no encumbrances or disputes.
  • Survey the property: Hire a licensed surveyor to confirm the boundaries and size.
  • Assess the building condition: Hire a structural engineer to inspect the building.
  • Review existing tenancies: If the property is already rented, review the lease agreements.
  • Check zoning: Ensure the property is zoned for commercial use.

Calculating Returns

The key metric for commercial real estate is the capitalization rate (cap rate):

Cap Rate = Annual Net Operating Income / Property Value

For example, if you buy an office building for ₦100 million and it generates ₦12 million in annual rental income (after expenses), your cap rate is 12%.

In Nigeria, commercial real estate cap rates typically range from 8% to 15%, depending on location, property type, and market conditions. Compare this to other investment options (fixed deposits at 10-15%, treasury bills at 15-20%) to determine if the investment makes sense.

Risks to Consider

Vacancy risk: If tenants leave and you can't replace them quickly, your income drops. Commercial leases are typically longer than residential (2-5 years), which provides stability but also means longer vacancy periods if a tenant leaves.

Maintenance costs: Commercial properties require more maintenance than residential. Elevators, generators, air conditioning systems, and common areas all need regular upkeep.

Market risk: Economic downturns can reduce demand for commercial space. The COVID-19 pandemic showed how quickly office demand can change.

Regulatory risk: Government policies, rent control laws, and tax changes can affect your returns.

Tips for Success

  • Location is everything: A mediocre property in a great location will outperform a great property in a mediocre location.
  • Build relationships with tenants: Good tenants are your best asset. Treat them well, respond to their needs, and they'll stay longer.
  • Keep properties well-maintained: A well-maintained property attracts better tenants and commands higher rents.
  • Diversify: Don't put all your money into one property or one type of commercial real estate.
  • Work with professionals: Estate surveyors, lawyers, and property managers are worth their fees.

Final Thoughts

Commercial real estate investment in Nigeria can generate excellent returns, but it's not a get-rich-quick scheme. It requires capital, patience, knowledge, and active management. Start with thorough research, buy in growing areas, and focus on long-term value creation.

The Nigerian commercial real estate market is growing. Urbanization, economic development, and a growing middle class are driving demand for quality commercial spaces. If you position yourself correctly, you can benefit from this growth for decades to come.
