The Guide to Starting a Transportation Business in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 9 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Moving people and goods from one place to another is the backbone of any economy. In Nigeria, with our massive population, growing urbanization, and expanding trade, the transportation business offers enormous opportunities. Whether you want to run a taxi service, start a haulage company, or launch a bus transport service, here's what you need to know.

Types of Transportation Businesses

Taxi and Ride-Hailing

With platforms like Uber, Bolt, and inDrive operating in major Nigerian cities, the taxi business has evolved. You can own vehicles and register them on these platforms, or start your own branded taxi service. A single Toyota Corolla or Camry costs between ₦5 million and ₦15 million (new) or ₦2 million to ₦6 million (used).

Bus Transport (Interstate and Intrastate)

Transporting passengers between cities or within cities. Luxury buses for Lagos-Abuja routes can generate ₦50,000 to ₦100,000+ per trip. Intrastate buses (like Lagos BRT-style services) serve daily commuters.

Haulage and Trucking

Moving goods across the country. This is one of the most profitable segments. A 30-ton truck can earn ₦300,000 to ₦800,000+ per trip depending on the route. Trucks cost between ₦15 million and ₦50 million+ new.

Delivery and Courier

Small-package delivery using motorcycles or vans. Lower capital requirement than haulage but growing rapidly with e-commerce.

School Bus Services

Transporting students to and from school. Contracts with schools provide steady, predictable income. Requires specially marked buses and trained drivers.

Legal Requirements

Every transportation business in Nigeria needs:

  • CAC registration: Register your business name or company.
  • Vehicle registration: All vehicles must be registered with the relevant state motor vehicle administration.
  • Hackney permit: For taxis and commercial vehicles, issued by the state transport authority.
  • Road worthiness certificate: Annual certification from the Vehicle Inspection Office (VIO).
  • Insurance: Comprehensive vehicle insurance and passenger/cargo insurance.
  • Driver's licenses: All drivers must have valid commercial driver's licenses.
  • State transport union registration: NURTW and other relevant unions.

For Haulage Specifically

  • National Transport Commission registration
  • Special haulage permits for oversized loads
  • Customs documentation if transporting imported goods

Acquiring Vehicles

You can buy new or used vehicles. New vehicles come with warranties but cost significantly more. Used vehicles (especially "Tokunbo" imports) are cheaper but require careful inspection.

For taxis, Toyota Corolla, Camry, and Yaris are the most popular because they're fuel-efficient, reliable, and spare parts are readily available.

For haulage, Mercedes-Benz, Volvo, and MAN trucks dominate the Nigerian market. They're expensive but built for heavy-duty operations.

For buses, Toyota Coaster, Kia Combi, and Yutong buses are common choices.

Hiring and Managing Drivers

Your drivers are the face of your business. Hire carefully:

  • Valid commercial driver's license
  • Minimum 3-5 years driving experience
  • Clean driving record (verify with previous employers)
  • Background check and guarantors
  • Drug testing

Compensation structures vary:

  • Salary plus commission: Base pay plus percentage of daily earnings
  • Target system: Driver pays a fixed daily amount to the owner and keeps the rest
  • Profit sharing: Percentage split of net revenue

The target system is most common in Nigeria, but it can encourage reckless driving if drivers push too hard to meet their targets. Monitor your drivers and prioritize safety over daily revenue.

Managing Operating Costs

Transportation businesses have significant operating costs:

  • Fuel: Your biggest ongoing expense. Fuel price fluctuations directly impact profitability.
  • Maintenance: Regular servicing, tire replacement, and repairs. Budget 10-15% of vehicle value annually for maintenance.
  • Insurance: Comprehensive insurance for each vehicle.
  • Driver salaries: Monthly or daily payments.
  • Union levies: NURTW and other union dues.
  • Permits and licenses: Annual renewals.

Technology and Tracking

Modern transportation businesses use technology to improve efficiency:

  • GPS tracking: Monitor vehicle locations in real-time. Prevents theft and unauthorized use.
  • Fleet management software: Track fuel consumption, maintenance schedules, and driver performance.
  • Booking platforms: For bus services, online booking systems make it easier for customers to purchase tickets.
  • Dashcams: For safety and evidence in case of accidents.

Startup Cost Estimate

For a small taxi business with 2 vehicles:

  • Vehicle purchase (2 used Toyota Corollas): ₦6 million - ₦12 million
  • Registration and permits: ₦200,000 - ₦500,000
  • Insurance: ₦300,000 - ₦600,000
  • GPS tracking and branding: ₦200,000 - ₦400,000
  • Working capital (3 months): ₦1 million - ₦2 million
  • Total: ₦7.7 million - ₦15.5 million

Final Thoughts

The transportation business in Nigeria is profitable but demanding. Vehicles break down. Drivers can be difficult. Fuel prices fluctuate. But with proper management, reliable vehicles, and good drivers, you can build a sustainable transportation business.

Start with one or two vehicles. Learn the business. Build systems. And scale gradually. The transportation sector will always be needed, and those who manage it well will always find customers.
