How to Start a Logistics and Delivery Business in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 8 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Nigeria's e-commerce boom has created an enormous demand for logistics and delivery services. Every online store, every restaurant doing delivery, every business sending packages needs a reliable delivery partner. And that's where you come in.

Starting a logistics business in Nigeria is more accessible than ever. You don't need a fleet registering understanding the transport sectoryour logistics companyof trucks to start. Many successful delivery companies began with a single motorcycle and grew from there.

Choose Your Delivery Model

Bike Delivery

The most common and affordable entry point. Motorcycles can navigate Lagos traffic, deliver within cities quickly, and cost less to operate than vans. A new delivery motorcycle costs between ₦800,000 and ₦1.5 million. You can start with one bike and scale up.

Van/Truck Delivery

For larger packages and interstate deliveries. A used delivery van costs between ₦3 million and ₦8 million. New ones are ₦10 million+. This model serves businesses that need to move bulk goods.

Tech-Enabled Platform

Build an app or website that connects senders with delivery riders. You don't need to own vehicles. You manage the platform and take a commission from each delivery. This requires technical expertise and marketing budget but scales faster.

Specialized Delivery

Focus on a niche: food delivery, pharmaceutical delivery, document courier, or fragile item handling. Specialization lets you charge premium prices and build expertise.

Register Your Business

Register with the CAC as a business name or limited company. For a logistics business, a limited company gives more credibility when dealing with corporate clients.

You'll also need:

  • NURTW registration: The National Union of Road Transport Workers regulates commercial transport operations.
  • State transport permits: Each state has its own transport authority.
  • Vehicle insurance: Comprehensive insurance for all your vehicles.
  • Goods-in-transit insurance: Covers packages you're delivering against loss or damage.

Get Your Vehicles

You can buy or lease vehicles. Buying gives you full control but requires more capital. Leasing reduces upfront costs but means ongoing payments.

For motorcycles, popular choices in Nigeria include Haojue, TVS, and using technology for fleet managementBajaj. They're fuel-efficient, durable, and spare parts are readily available.

For vans, Toyota HiAce and Volkswagen Transporter are the most common. They're reliable and mechanics across Nigeria know how to fix them.

Set Up Your Operations

Tracking System

Customers want to know where their packages are. Invest in a tracking system. This can be as simple as WhatsApp updates or as sophisticated as a GPS-tracked dashboard. Tech-enabled tracking is becoming the standard, and customers expect it.

Pricing Structure

Set clear prices based on:

  • Distance (within same area, across city, interstate)
  • Package size and weight
  • Delivery speed (same-day, next-day, standard)
  • Special handling requirements

Typical same-day delivery prices in Lagos range from ₦1,500 to ₦5,000 depending on distance. Interstate deliveries range from ₦3,000 to ₦15,000+.

Delivery Riders

Hire reliable riders with valid licenses. Conduct background checks. Require guarantors. Train them on customer service, package handling, and safety. Your riders are the face of your business. How they treat customers determines whether those customers come back.

Consider a compensation structure that combines base pay with per-delivery bonuses. This motivates riders to complete more deliveries while ensuring they earn a fair income.

Office or Hub

You need a central location for package pickup, sorting, and dispatch. This doesn't need to be fancy. A small office or warehouse in a central location works. What matters is that it's accessible to your riders and customers.

Find Your First Clients

Start with businesses that need regular delivery services:

  • Online stores: Instagram vendors, Jumia/Konga sellers, and e-commerce websites.
  • Restaurants: Especially those without their own delivery fleet.
  • Pharmacies: Medicine delivery is growing rapidly.
  • Offices: Document and parcel delivery between offices.
  • Individuals: People sending packages to friends and family.

Offer competitive introductory rates to win your first clients. Once they experience your reliability, they'll stay even if you raise prices later.

Startup Costs

For a small bike delivery operation in Lagos:

  • Business registration: ₦30,000 - ₦50,000
  • Delivery motorcycle: ₦800,000 - ₦1.5 million
  • Delivery boxes and branding: ₦50,000 - ₦100,000
  • Insurance: ₦100,000 - ₦200,000
  • Office/hub rent: ₦300,000 - ₦800,000
  • Marketing: ₦100,000 - ₦300,000
  • Working capital (3 months): ₦500,000 - ₦1 million
  • Total: ₦1.9 million - ₦4 million

Common Challenges and How to Handle Them

Traffic: Lagos traffic is legendary. Use motorcycles for city deliveries. Plan routes carefully. Use traffic data apps to find the fastest routes.

Rider management: Riders are your biggest operational challenge. Pay them well, treat them fairly, and have clear rules about package handling and customer interaction.

Lost or damaged packages: Have a clear claims process. Insurance helps, but prevention is better. Train riders on proper handling and use secure packaging.

Competition: The delivery space is crowded. Differentiate through reliability, customer service, and technology. Be the company that actually delivers on time.

Final Thoughts

The logistics and delivery business in Nigeria is growing rapidly. E-commerce is expanding, more businesses are going online, and customers expect fast, reliable delivery. If you can provide that consistently, you'll build a profitable business.

Start small. Focus on reliability. Treat your riders well. And scale as demand grows. The companies that win in this space are the ones that deliver on their promises, every single time.
