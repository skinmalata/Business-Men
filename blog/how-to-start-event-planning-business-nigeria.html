How to Start an Event Planning Business in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 8 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Nigerians love to celebrate. Weddings, birthdays, anniversaries, corporate events, product launches, church programs, traditional ceremonies. The events industry in Nigeria is worth billions of naira, and it keeps growing.

If you're organized, creative, and good with people, event planning can be a rewarding and profitable business. But it's not all glamour and party photos. Behind every beautiful event is hours of planning, coordination, stress management, and problem-solving.

Choose Your Event Niche

The events industry is broad. Specializing helps you stand out:

Weddings

The biggest segment of Nigeria's events market. Nigerian weddings are elaborate, multi-day affairs with big budgets. A wedding planner in Lagos can charge ₦500,000 to ₦5 million+ per event depending on the scale. But competition is fierce, and clients have high expectations.

Corporate Events

Conferences, product launches, seminars, award ceremonies, and company parties. Corporate clients pay well and provide repeat business. They also expect professionalism, punctuality, and detailed reporting.

Social Events

Birthday parties, anniversaries, baby showers, graduations. These are smaller than weddings but more frequent. Good for building a steady client base.

Funeral Events

A sensitive but significant segment. Funeral planning in Nigeria involves coordinating venue, catering, printing, transportation, and entertainment. It requires tact and cultural sensitivity.

Register Your Business

Register with the CAC. A business name is fine to start, but a limited company gives more credibility for corporate contracts.

Consider joining professional bodies like the Association of Professional Event Planners Nigeria (APEPN) for networking, training, and credibility.

Build Your Vendor Network

As an event planner, you don't do everything yourself. You coordinate vendors. Your value is in knowing the best vendors, negotiating good prices, and ensuring they deliver.

Build relationships with:

  • Venues (event halls, hotels, gardens)
  • Caterers
  • Decorators and florists
  • Photographers and videographers
  • DJs and MCs
  • Sound and lighting technicians
  • Printers (invitations, banners, programs)
  • Transportation companies
  • Entertainment (bands, dancers, comedians)
  • Cake bakers
  • Security companies

Test each vendor before recommending them to clients. Attend their events. Check their work. Your reputation depends on the quality of vendors you bring to the table.

Set Your Pricing

Event planners in Nigeria typically charge in one of these ways:

  • Flat fee: A fixed amount for the entire event (₦200,000 to ₦5 million+ depending on scale)
  • Percentage of event budget: Usually 10-20% of the total event cost
  • Hourly rate: ₦10,000 to ₦50,000 per hour for consultation and planning
  • Package pricing: Different packages (basic, standard, premium) with different services included

Be transparent about your pricing. Clients should know exactly what they're paying for and what's included.

Get the Right Tools

You don't need a physical office to start, but you do need:

  • Planning software: Tools like Excel, Google Sheets, or event planning apps to manage timelines, budgets, and vendor contacts.
  • A portfolio: Photos of events you've planned. If you're just starting, offer to plan a few events at reduced rates or for free to build your portfolio.
  • A professional website: Showcase your services, portfolio, and testimonials.
  • Social media presence: Instagram is essential for event planners in Nigeria. Post beautiful photos and behind-the-scenes content.
  • Transportation: You'll be running around the city meeting vendors, visiting venues, and supervising events.

Market Your Services

Event planning is a visual business. Your marketing should showcase your work:

  • Instagram: Post high-quality photos and videos of your events. Use relevant hashtags like #LagosWeddingPlanner, #AbujaEvents, #NigerianWedding.
  • Referrals: Happy clients are your best marketing. Ask for referrals and offer incentives.
  • Collaborations: Partner with photographers, decorators, and venues. They'll recommend you to their clients.
  • Wedding fairs and exhibitions: Set up a booth at events like the Lagos Wedding Fair to meet potential clients.
  • Business directories: List on BusinessMen so people searching for event planners can find you.

Common Challenges and How to Handle Them

Difficult clients: Some clients have unrealistic expectations or change their minds constantly. Set clear boundaries from the beginning. Put everything in writing. Have a contract that specifies the number of revisions allowed.

Vendor issues: Vendors who don't show up, deliver late, or provide substandard service. Always have backup vendors. And never work with a vendor you haven't tested.

Cash flow: Event planning often requires you to pay vendors before the client pays you. Structure your payment terms so you receive at least 50% upfront and the balance before the event.

Stress and burnout: Events are high-pressure. Learn to delegate. Build a team. And take breaks between events.

Startup Cost Estimate

For a small event planning business:

  • Business registration: ₦30,000 - ₦50,000
  • Website and branding: ₦100,000 - ₦300,000
  • Planning tools and software: ₦50,000 - ₦100,000
  • Marketing and portfolio building: ₦100,000 - ₦300,000
  • Working capital: ₦200,000 - ₦500,000
  • Total: ₦480,000 - ₦1.25 million

Final Thoughts

The event planning business in Nigeria is competitive but rewarding. Success comes from creativity, reliability, and exceptional client service. Every event you plan is a walking advertisement for your business. Make it count.

Start with small events, build your portfolio, grow your vendor network, and gradually take on bigger projects. The Nigerian events market is growing, and there's always room for planners who deliver excellence.
