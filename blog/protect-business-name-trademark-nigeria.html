How to Protect Your Business Name and Trademark in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 6 min read Updated May 20, 2026

You've spent months building your brand. You picked the perfect name. You designed a logo that looks professional. You've been using it on your shop sign, your receipts, your Instagram page. Then one day, you discover another business in a neighboring state is using the exact same name and logo.

What can you do? If you never registered your trademark, very little. And that's a painful lesson too many Nigerian business owners learn the hard way.

The Difference Between Business Name Registration and Trademark

Many people think that registering their business name with the CAC protects their brand. It doesn't. Not really.

CAC registration simply means nobody else can register the exact same business name with the CAC. But it doesn't stop someone from using your name as a trading name, or from using your logo, or from building a brand that looks and sounds just like yours.

A trademark is different. A trademark is a legal right that gives you exclusive use of a name, logo, slogan, or combination of these for specific categories of goods or services. When you trademark your brand, you can legally stop anyone else from using it in Nigeria.

What Can You Trademark?

In Nigeria, you can trademark:

  • Business names and brand names (like "Dangote" or "GTBank")
  • Logos and symbols (like the MTN yellow logo or the Zenith Bank emblem)
  • Slogans and taglines (though these are harder to register)
  • Product names (like "Indomie" or "Peak Milk")
  • Sound marks (like the MTN jingle)
  • Color combinations associated with your brand

How to Register a Trademark in Nigeria

Trademarks in Nigeria are handled by the Trademarks, Patents and Designs Registry, which is part of the Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment. Here's the process:

Step 1: Conduct a Trademark Search

Before you apply, you need to check if your trademark is already registered. You can do this at the Trademarks Registry in Abuja or through a trademark agent. The search costs around ₦5,000 to ₦10,000.

This step is critical. If someone already owns the trademark you want, your application will be rejected and you'll lose your application fees.

Step 2: File the Application

You'll need to submit:

  • A completed trademark application form
  • A clear representation of your trademark (logo, name, or both)
  • A list of the goods or services the trademark will cover (classified according to the Nice Classification system)
  • The prescribed fee (approximately ₦30,000 to ₦50,000 depending on the number of classes)

Most people use a trademark agent or intellectual property lawyer for this. The registry can be bureaucratic, and an experienced agent knows how to navigate the process efficiently.

Step 3: Acceptance and Publication

Once your application is accepted, it gets published in the Trademarks Journal. This gives other parties 2 months to oppose your registration if they believe it conflicts with their existing rights.

If nobody opposes, or if any opposition is resolved in your favor, your trademark proceeds to registration.

Step 4: Certification

After the opposition period passes without issues, you'll receive your trademark certificate. The entire process typically takes 6 to 12 months, sometimes longer if there are complications.

How Much Does It Cost?

Here's a rough breakdown:

  • Trademark search: ₦5,000 - ₦10,000
  • Application and registration (per class): ₦30,000 - ₦50,000
  • Agent fees: ₦50,000 - ₦150,000
  • Total estimated cost: ₦85,000 - ₦210,000

It's not cheap, but compare it to the cost of rebranding your entire business because someone else registered your name first. Or the legal fees you'd spend trying to fight someone in court without a registered trademark.

Trademark Classes Explained

Trademarks are registered under specific classes. There are 45 classes in total. Class 1-34 cover goods, and Class 35-45 cover services.

For example, if you're running a restaurant, you'd register under Class 43 (restaurant services). If you also sell packaged food products, you'd add Class 30 (food products). Each additional class costs extra.

Don't register under classes you don't need. But do think about your future plans. If you plan to expand from selling clothes (Class 25) to opening a fashion school (Class 41), register both classes now. It's cheaper than filing a separate application later.

How Long Does a Trademark Last?

A registered trademark in Nigeria is valid for 7 years from the date of registration. After that, you can renew it for periods of 14 years at a time. Renewal fees are relatively small compared to the initial registration cost.

Mark your renewal dates. If you let your trademark expire and someone else registers it, you'll have to fight to get it back.

Enforcing Your Trademark Rights

Once your trademark is registered, you have the legal right to:

  • Sue anyone who uses your trademark without permission
  • Request that customs seize counterfeit goods bearing your trademark
  • License your trademark to others for a fee (franchising)
  • Sell your trademark as part of a business sale

If you find someone infringing on your trademark, start with a cease-and-desist letter from your lawyer. Most infringers will stop when they realize you have a registered trademark and mean business. If they don't, you can take legal action.

What If Someone Already Registered Your Name?

If you discover that someone else has already registered a trademark similar to yours, you have a few options:

  • Rebrand: It's painful, but sometimes it's the cheapest and fastest solution.
  • Negotiate: You might be able to buy the trademark from the current owner.
  • Challenge the registration: If you can prove you used the mark before they registered it, or if their registration was done in bad faith, you can apply to have it cancelled. This requires a lawyer and can be expensive.

Final Advice

If your brand matters to you, trademark it. If you're planning to scale, franchise, or attract investors, trademark it. If you've invested serious money in marketing and building recognition around a name or logo, trademark it.

The cost of trademark registration is a fraction of what you'd spend on rebranding or litigation. And in Nigeria's increasingly competitive market, your brand is one of your most valuable assets. Protect it.
