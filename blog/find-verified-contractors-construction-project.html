How to Find Verified Contractors for Your Construction Project

By BusinessMen Editorial 7 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Building a house in Nigeria is one of the biggest investments you'll ever make. And the contractor you choose will determine whether that investment becomes your dream home or a never-ending money pit with cracked walls and leaking roofs.

I've heard too many stories of people who paid contractors upfront, only to find the contractor disappeared with the money. Let me help you avoid becoming one of those stories.

Where to Find Contractors

Business Directories

Start with online business directories like BusinessMen where construction companies list their services, contact information, and sometimes customer reviews. This gives you a starting list of contractors operating in your area.

Referrals from Friends and Family

Ask people who have recently built or renovated. A contractor who did a good job for your cousin is more likely to do a good job for you. But don't stop at the referral. Still do your own due diligence.

Nigerian Institute of Building (NIOB)

The NIOB maintains a register of qualified building professionals. You can contact your state chapter for recommendations of registered and certified contractors.

Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)

Verify that any contractor you're considering is a registered business. You can search the CAC database to confirm their registration status. An unregistered contractor is a red flag.

How to Vet a Construction Contractor

1. Check Their Registration and Licenses

A legitimate construction company should be registered with the CAC. They should also be registered with relevant professional bodies like the Council for the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria (COREN) if they offer engineering services, or the Nigerian Institute of Building.

Ask to see their registration certificates. A verifying any business before hiringlegitimate contractor won't hesitate to show them.

2. Review Their Portfolio

Ask to see projects they've completed. Better yet, ask to visit some of those projects. Talk to the building owners. Ask about the contractor's work quality, timeliness, communication, and whether the project stayed within budget.

Pay attention to the age of the buildings they've constructed. A building that looks good after 5 years tells you more than a brand new one.

3. Get Multiple Quotes

Get detailed quotes from at least three contractors. The quote should break down costs by category: foundation, block work, roofing, plumbing, electrical, finishing, and so on.

Don't automatically go with the cheapest quote. A quote that's significantly lower than others might mean the contractor is cutting corners or planning to add hidden costs later.

4. Check References

Ask for at least three references from recent clients. Call them. Ask specific questions:

  • Did the project finish on time?
  • Did the final cost match the quoted price?
  • Were there any quality issues?
  • How was the contractor's communication?
  • Would you hire them again?

5. Visit Their Office

A contractor with a proper office is more likely to be legitimate than one who operates solely from a phone number. Visit their office. Meet the team. See if they have the equipment and resources to handle your project.

6. Verify Their Financial Capacity

Can the contractor afford to start your project? Some contractors take on more projects than they can handle financially, leading to delays as they wait for money from one project to fund another. Ask about their current workload and financial capacity.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Demanding full payment upfront: No legitimate contractor expects 100% payment before starting. Standard practice is staged payments tied to project milestones.
  • No written contract: Never, ever start a construction project without a detailed written contract.
  • Unwillingness to provide references: If they can't or won't give you references, walk away.
  • No physical address: A contractor without an office is a risk.
  • Pressure to decide immediately: Legitimate contractors understand that building is a big decision and won't pressure you.
  • Vague quotes: "₦15 million for the whole thing" without a breakdown is unacceptable.

The Contract: What It Must Include

Your construction contract should clearly state:

  • Scope of work (exactly what the contractor will do)
  • Materials to be used (specify brands and quality where possible)
  • Project timeline with milestones
  • Payment schedule tied to milestones
  • Penalty clauses for delays
  • Warranty period for defects
  • Dispute resolution process
  • Termination conditions

Have a lawyer review the contract before you sign. The ₦50,000 to ₦100,000 you spend on legal review could save you millions in disputes.

Payment Best Practices

Never pay the full amount upfront. A common payment structure in Nigeria is:

  • 15-20% mobilization fee to start
  • Progressive payments at each milestone (foundation, block work, roofing, etc.)
  • 5-10% retention held until after the defect liability period (usually 6-12 months)

Always pay into the company's corporate bank account, not a personal account. And keep receipts for every payment.

Final Thoughts

Finding the right contractor takes time and effort, but it's the most important decision you'll make in your construction project. Don't rush it. Don't skip the verification steps. And don't let anyone pressure you into making a decision you're not comfortable with.

Your building will stand for decades. The extra weeks you spend finding the right contractor are worth it.
