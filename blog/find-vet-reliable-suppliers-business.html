How to Find and Vet Reliable Suppliers for Your Business

By BusinessMen Editorial 7 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Your suppliers can make or break your business. A reliable supplier delivers quality products on time at fair prices. An unreliable one sends substandard goods late, raises prices without notice, or disappears with your deposit.

Whether you're running a restaurant in Abuja that needs consistent food supplies, a fashion brand in Lagos sourcing fabrics, or a construction negotiating better deals once you find themcompany in Port Harcourt buying building materials, finding the right suppliers is one of the most important business decisions you'll make.

Where to Find Suppliers

Trade Fairs and Exhibitions

Nigeria hosts several trade fairs throughout the year, including the Lagos International Trade Fair, Abuja International Trade Fair, and industry-specific exhibitions. These events bring together suppliers and buyers under one roof. You can see products firsthand, compare prices, and build relationships.

Online Business Directories

Platforms like BusinessMen list suppliers across various industries with contact details, locations, and customer reviews. You can filter by product category and location to find suppliers near you.

Industry Associations

Most industries have associations that maintain member directories. The Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN), the Nigerian Association of Chambers of Commerce, Industry, Mines and Agriculture (NACCIMA), and sector-specific groups can connect you with verified suppliers.

Referrals from Other Business Owners

Ask other business owners in your industry who they use. A supplier who serves multiple businesses in your sector has proven reliability. But don't rely on just one referral. Get multiple opinions.

Markets and Industrial Clusters

Visit major markets in your area. In Lagos, Idumota and Trade Fair Complex are hubs for various goods. In Kano, Kantin Kwari is famous for textiles. In Onitsha, the main market is one of West Africa's largest. Going in person lets you assess quality and negotiate directly.

How to Vet a Supplier

1. Verify Their Business Registration

Check that the supplier is registered with the CAC. Ask for their RC or BN number and verify it. A supplier that isn't registered has no legal standing, and you have limited recourse if things go wrong.

2. Request Product Samples

Before committing to a large order, request samples. Test the quality yourself. If you're a restaurant, taste the ingredients. If you're a fashion brand, feel the fabric and test how it sews. If you're in construction, test the strength of the materials.

Don't skip this step. A supplier might show you premium samples and deliver substandard products. Order a small trial batch first before going big.

3. Check Their Delivery Track Record

Ask about their delivery timelines and reliability. Do they deliver on time? What happens when there are delays? How do they handle damaged goods during transit?

Request references from their current customers and actually call them. Ask specifically about delivery reliability and how the supplier handles problems.

4. Compare Pricing

Get quotes from at least three suppliers for the same products. Compare not just the unit price but the total cost including delivery, minimum order quantities, and payment terms.

The cheapest supplier isn't always the best. If a supplier's prices are significantly lower than the market rate, ask why. They might be cutting corners on quality or planning to raise prices later.

5. Assess Their Financial Stability

A supplier with cash flow problems can't maintain consistent inventory. They might not be able to fulfill your orders when you need them. Look for signs of financial stability: a proper office, adequate inventory, professional staff, and a history of consistent operations.

6. Evaluate Their Communication

How responsive is the supplier? Do they answer your calls and emails promptly? Do they communicate proactively about delays or issues? Good communication is a strong indicator of a reliable business partner.

Building Strong Supplier Relationships

Once you've found good suppliers, nurture those relationships:

  • Pay on time: Nothing damages a supplier relationship faster than late payments. If you agree on 30-day payment terms, pay within 30 days.
  • Communicate clearly: Give accurate forecasts of your needs. If you expect a busy season, let your supplier know in advance so they can prepare.
  • Be loyal but not dependent: Stick with reliable suppliers, but always have a backup. Don't put all your eggs in one basket.
  • Negotiate fairly: Push for good prices, but don't squeeze your suppliers so hard that they can't make a profit. A supplier who can't make money won't stay in business.
  • Visit their facility: Regular visits show you're serious about the relationship and give you insight into their operations.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • No physical address: A supplier without a verifiable location is risky.
  • Unwillingness to provide samples: If they won't let you test their products before buying, walk away.
  • Demanding full payment upfront: Standard practice is a deposit with balance on delivery.
  • Inconsistent quality: If the first batch is great but the second is terrible, they're not reliable.
  • Poor communication: If they're hard to reach before you've paid, they'll be impossible to reach after.
  • No references: A legitimate supplier should be able to provide references from satisfied customers.

Final Thoughts

Finding and vetting reliable suppliers takes time and effort, but it's one of the best investments you can make in your business. A good supplier relationship is a competitive advantage that's hard to replicate.

Do your homework. Start small. Build trust over time. And always have a backup plan. Your supply chain is the backbone of your business. Treat it accordingly.
