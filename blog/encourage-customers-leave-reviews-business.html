How to Encourage Customers to Leave Reviews for Your Business

By BusinessMen Editorial 5 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Happy customers don't always leave reviews. In fact, studies show that satisfied customers are less likely to leave reviews than dissatisfied ones. A customer who had a great experience might think "that was nice" and move on. A customer who had a bad experience is much more motivated to share their frustration.

This means that if you're not actively encouraging reviews, your online profile will be skewed toward negative feedback. Here's how to get more positive reviews from your satisfied customers.

1. Ask at the Right Time

Timing is everything. Ask for a review when the customer is happiest, which is usually immediately after a successful transaction or service delivery.

For a restaurant, ask as they're leaving or include a note with the bill. For a service business, ask after the job is completed and the customer confirms they're satisfied. For an online store, send a follow-up message 2-3 days after delivery when they've had time to use the product.

2. Make It Easy

The easier it is to leave a review, the more likely customers are to do it. Provide direct links to your review pages. Don't make customers search for where to leave a review.

Send a WhatsApp message with a direct link: "Hi Adebayo, thank you for choosing us! If you have a moment, we'd love to hear about your experience. Please leave us a review here: [link]."

For in-person businesses, display a QR code at your counter that customers can scan to go directly to your review page.

3. Ask Personally

A personal request is more effective than a generic one. When you interact with a customer and they express satisfaction, that's the perfect moment to ask.

"I'm so glad you're happy with the service! Would you mind leaving us a quick review? It really helps our business."

Personal requests feel genuine and customers are more likely to respond positively.

4. Use Multiple Channels

Don't rely on just one method to ask for reviews. Use a combination of:

  • WhatsApp messages: The most effective channel in Nigeria
  • Email: For businesses that collect customer emails
  • SMS: For quick, direct requests
  • In-person requests: For businesses with physical locations
  • Social media: Post occasionally asking followers to share their experiences
  • Receipts and packaging: Include a note asking for reviews

5. Respond to Existing Reviews

When customers see that you respond to reviews, they're more likely to leave one. It shows that you read reviews and value customer feedback. Respond to every review, positive or negative, and watch your review volume increase.

6. Create a Review Culture

Make asking for reviews a standard part of your customer service process. Train your staff to ask for reviews at the right moment. Include it in your post-service follow-up routine. Make it a habit, not an afterthought.

7. Showcase Your Reviews

When customers see that other people have left reviews, they're more likely to do the same. Display your best reviews on your social media, website, and in your physical location. Share review milestones: "Thank you to our 100th reviewer!"

8. Don't Offer Incentives for Reviews

While it might be tempting to offer discounts or freebies in exchange for reviews, most review platforms prohibit this practice. It can also reduce the credibility of your reviews. Customers can tell when a review was incentivized, and it diminishes the value of the review.

Instead of offering incentives, focus on providing exceptional service that naturally inspires customers to leave reviews.

How Often Should You Ask?

Don't overdo it. Asking once after a transaction is appropriate. Asking multiple times is annoying and can backfire. If a customer doesn't respond to your first request, don't ask again. They'll leave a review if they want to.

For repeat customers, you can ask periodically (every few months) but always be respectful of their time and don't make it feel like an obligation.

What If You Get a Negative Review?

Don't let the fear of negative reviews stop you from asking for reviews. Negative reviews are inevitable, and they're not the end of the world. A mix of positive and negative reviews looks more authentic than a perfect 5-star profile.

Focus on providing excellent service, and the positive reviews will outweigh the negative ones. When negative reviews do come, respond professionally and use them as opportunities to improve.

Final Thoughts

Getting more reviews is simple: ask. But asking effectively requires timing, ease, and genuine care for your customers' experiences. Make it a regular part of your business routine, and over time, your review profile will grow into one of your strongest marketing assets.

Start today. Think about your last satisfied customer. Send them a message asking for a review. Then do it again with the next one. Consistency is key.
