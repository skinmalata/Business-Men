How to Register a Business in Nigeria: Complete Step-by-Step Guide

By BusinessMen Editorial 10 min read Updated May 20, 2026

So you've finally decided to make it official. You're tired of telling people you run a business and watching them ask for your RC number. It's time to register.

Registering your business in Nigeria isn't as complicated as people make it sound. Yes, there's paperwork. Yes, you'll deal with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). understanding protecting your business name and trademarkNigerian tax obligationsBut thousands of entrepreneurs do it every month, and you can too. This guide walks you through every single step, no shortcuts, no vague instructions.

Why You Need to Register Your Business

Let's get this out of the way first. Some people think they can just operate without registration and save money. Sure, you can. Until a client asks for your CAC certificate to process payment. Until you try to open a corporate bank account and the bank tells you they need your registration documents. Until you want to apply for a government contract or a business loan.

A registered business gives you credibility. It separates you from the thousands of informal operators. It also protects your business name so nobody else can register and use it. Think about it, you've been building your brand in your head for months. You don't want someone in Abuja to wake up one morning and register the exact name you've been using.

Types of Business Registration in Nigeria

Before you start, you need to know what type of registration fits your business. The CAC offers a few options:

Business Name Registration

This is the most common and cheapest option. It's perfect for sole proprietorships and partnerships. If you're running a small shop in Ikeja, a freelance consulting business in Enugu, or a tailoring business in Kano, this is likely what you need. Registration costs around ₦20,000 to ₦30,000 depending on whether you do it yourself or use an accredited agent.

Limited Liability Company (Ltd)

If you're planning to scale, bring in investors, or bid for serious contracts, you'll want a limited liability company. This structure separates your personal assets from the business. If things go wrong, your personal house and car are protected. The minimum share capital is ₦100,000, though you don't necessarily need to deposit that money anywhere. Registration costs between ₦50,000 and ₦100,000.

Company Limited by Guarantee

This is for non-profit organizations, NGOs, and charities. If you're setting up a foundation or a community development organization, this is your route.

Step 1: Choose and Reserve Your Business Name

Everything starts with a name. And not just any name, it has to be unique. The CAC won't register a name that's already taken or too similar to an existing registered business.

Head to the CAC portal at cac.gov.ng and create an account on the Companies Registration Portal (CRP). From there, you can search for your desired business name. Try at least two or three variations because your first choice might already be taken.

Here's a practical tip: avoid names that are too generic. "Lagos Trading Company" will almost certainly be rejected. But "Lagos Premium Trading Company" or "Lagos Premium Trading Enterprises" might pass. The CAC wants names that are distinctive.

Once you find an available name, you can reserve it. The name reservation costs about ₦500 and holds the name for you while you prepare the rest of your documents.

Step 2: Prepare Your Documents

For a business name registration, you'll need:

  • A valid form of identification (National ID, International Passport, or Driver's License)
  • Passport photograph
  • Residential address
  • Business address (this can be your home address if you're working from home)
  • Nature of business (what your business does)
  • Email address and phone number

If you're registering a limited liability company, the requirements are more extensive. You'll need:

  • Memorandum and Articles of Association (MEMART)
  • Details of shareholders and their share allocation
  • Details of directors (minimum of two for a private limited company)
  • Details of a company secretary
  • Registered address of the company
  • Statement of share capital

    • Most people use an accredited CAC agent to prepare the MEMART. It costs extra, but it saves you from headaches. You can find accredited agents on the CAC website or through recommendations from other business owners.

    Step 3: Complete the Online Application

    Log back into the CAC portal and fill out the registration form. For a business name, you'll complete the BN-1 form. For a company, it's the CAC 1.1 form (though the online system handles most of this automatically now).

    Upload your documents, fill in all the required fields, and double-check everything. A wrong date of birth or misspelled name can cause delays. Trust me, I've seen people wait an extra two weeks because of a typo.

    Pay the registration fee online. The CAC accepts debit card payments. For a business name, expect to pay around ₦20,000 to ₦30,000. For a limited company, it's ₦50,000 to ₦100,000 depending on share capital.

    Step 4: Wait for Approval

    After submission, the CAC will review your application. This usually takes between 3 to 7 working days for business name registration. For companies, it can take 1 to 3 weeks, especially if there are queries from the CAC officers.

    You'll receive email notifications about the status of your application. If there are issues, they'll tell you what needs to be corrected. Fix it and resubmit as quickly as possible.

    Step 5: Collect Your Certificate

    Once approved, you'll receive your CAC certificate digitally. The CAC now issues electronic certificates, which are just as valid as the old paper ones. You can download it from the portal and print as many copies as you need.

    If you prefer a physical copy, you can visit the nearest CAC office. The main office is in Abuja, but there are state offices across the country including Lagos, Port Harcourt, Kano, and Ibadan.

    What to Do After Registration

    Getting your certificate is just the beginning. Here's what you should do next:

    • Open a corporate bank account: Take your CAC certificate to any bank and open a business account. Don't mix personal and business finances.
    • Register for taxes: Head to the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) for your Tax Identification Number (TIN). You'll need this for everything from filing returns to bidding for contracts.
    • Get necessary permits: Depending on your business type, you may need additional licenses. A restaurant needs NAFDAC approval. A pharmacy needs PCN registration. A transport company needs state transport permits.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    I've seen people mess up their registration in ways that cost them time and money. Here are the biggest mistakes:

    Using a name that's too similar to an existing business. The CAC will reject it, and you'll waste your application fee. Always search thoroughly before applying.

    Not checking name availability on social media. Even if the CAC approves your name, you want to make sure you can get matching handles on Instagram, Twitter, and Facebook. Check before you commit.

    Skipping the TIN registration. Your CAC registration automatically generates a TIN now, but you still need to activate it with FIRS. Don't skip this step.

    Not renewing annual returns. Yes, even business names need to file annual returns. It's a small fee, but failing to do it can result in penalties and your business being struck off the register.

    Final Thoughts

    Registering your business in Nigeria is a straightforward process if you follow the steps. The CAC has made things much easier with their online portal. You don't need to travel to Abuja. You don't need to know someone inside. You just need patience, the right documents, and a bit of money.

    The real question isn't whether you can afford to register your business. It's whether you can afford not to. Every day you operate without registration, you're limiting your growth potential. Banks won't take you seriously. Big clients won't trust you. And you'll always be looking over your shoulder wondering if someone will register your business name before you do.
