Top 10 Things to Look for When Choosing a Hotel in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 6 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Booking a hotel in Nigeria can feel like a gamble. The photos online look gorgeous, the price seems reasonable, and then you arrive to find a room that looks nothing like the pictures, no hot water, and a generator that switches off at midnight.

1. Location and Accessibility

Where the hotel is located matters more than you think. In Lagos, a hotel on the Island might be perfect for business meetings in Victoria Island but a nightmare if you need to get to Ikeja for a 7 AM appointment. Traffic on the Third Mainland Bridge at rush hour is no joke.

Check the hotel's proximity to where you need to be. Look at the neighborhood. Is it in a safe, well-lit area? Is it arranging transport to your hotelclose to restaurants, pharmacies, and ATMs? In cities like Abuja and Port Harcourt, location also affects how easy it is to get airport transfers.

2. Security

Security should be your number one priority. Look for hotels with:

  • 24-hour security personnel at the entrance
  • CCTV cameras in common areas
  • Controlled access to guest floors (key card or code entry)
  • In-room safes for valuables
  • Adequate lighting in parking areas

Don't be shy about asking about security measures when you call to book. A reputable hotel will be happy to tell you about their security protocols.

3. Power Supply

This is Nigeria. Power supply matters. Ask specifically about their generator backup. Do they have 24-hour power? Does the generator cover air conditioning or just lights and fans? Some hotels run generators only until midnight, and you don't want to discover that at 1 AM in July heat.

Many upscale hotels in Lagos and Abuja now have solar power systems as backup, which is a good sign of investment in reliable infrastructure.

4. Water Supply and Quality

Running water isn't guaranteed everywhere. Check reviews specifically mentioning water supply. Does the hotel have a borehole? Is there hot water? Is the water pressure adequate?

If you're sensitive to water quality, ask whether the hotel has water treatment systems. Some hotels provide bottled drinking water in rooms, which is a nice touch.

5. Cleanliness and Hygiene

Photos can be misleading. Look at recent guest reviews on Google, TripAdvisor, or Booking.com. Pay special attention to reviews from the last three months. A hotel that was clean two years ago might not be today.

When you arrive, check the bathroom first. It's the best indicator of overall cleanliness. Look at the sheets, the floor, and behind furniture. If the bathroom isn't spotless, the rest of the room probably isn't either.

6. Internet Connectivity

If you're traveling for business or just want to stay connected, reliable Wi-Fi is essential. Ask about the internet speed and whether it's free. Some Nigerian hotels charge extra for Wi-Fi, and the ones that offer it free sometimes have connections so slow you can barely load a webpage.

Business hotels in areas like Victoria Island, Lekki, and Maitama generally have better internet infrastructure.

7. Food and Dining Options

Does the hotel have a restaurant? What are the operating hours? If you're arriving late at night, will you be able to get food? Room service availability is important, especially if you're traveling with children or have dietary restrictions.

Also check whether the hotel allows outside food delivery. Some hotels in Nigeria restrict food delivery services, which can be inconvenient if you want something specific from a local restaurant.

8. Reviews and Reputation

Read reviews from multiple sources. Don't rely on just one platform. Look for patterns in reviews. If multiple people complain about the same thing (no hot water, rude staff, noisy rooms), it's probably a real issue.

Also look at how the hotel responds to negative reviews. A hotel that responds professionally and addresses concerns shows they care about their reputation. One that ignores complaints or responds defensively is a red flag.

9. Value for Money

Price isn't everything, but it matters. A ₦80,000 per night hotel should deliver significantly more than a ₦25,000 per night one. Compare what's included: breakfast, Wi-Fi, airport pickup, gym access, pool.

Some hotels in Nigeria offer better value during weekdays when business travelers are fewer, while others have weekend discounts. Ask about promotions and corporate rates if you're traveling for work.

10. Customer Service

How the hotel treats you from the first phone call tells you a lot. Are they responsive? Do they answer your questions clearly? Are they flexible with special requests?

Good customer service can make up for minor shortcomings. A hotel with average facilities but excellent staff will often provide a better experience than a fancy hotel with indifferent employees.

Red Flags to Watch Out For

  • Prices that seem too good to be true: They usually are. A "luxury" hotel room at ₦10,000 per night in Lagos is suspicious.
  • No recent reviews: If the last review is from two years ago, something might be wrong.
  • Refusal to share photos of actual rooms: Stock photos are not the same as real room photos.
  • No phone number or physical address: Legitimate hotels have both.
  • Payment only through personal bank accounts: Hotels should have corporate accounts.

Final Tips

Book directly with the hotel when possible. You'll often get better rates and more flexibility than booking through third-party platforms. Call ahead to confirm your reservation, especially during peak seasons like December when hotels across Nigeria fill up quickly.

And if you find a hotel you love, leave a review. Your feedback helps other travelers make better decisions and encourages hotels to maintain their standards.
