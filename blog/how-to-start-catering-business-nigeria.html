How to Start a Catering Business: Complete Guide

By BusinessMen Editorial 8 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Nigerians celebrate with food. Every wedding, birthday, funeral, corporate event, and church program needs catering. The catering business in Nigeria is consistently profitable because events never stop happening. If you can cook well and manage logistics, this business can serve you for life.

Choose Your Catering Niche

Event Catering

The most common type. Catering weddings, birthdays, corporate events, and parties. This requires the ability to cook in large quantities, manage a team, and deliver food to event venues on time.

Corporate Catering

Providing daily or weekly meals for offices. This is contract-based and provides steady, predictable income. Companies in Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt are always looking for reliable corporate caterers.

Home Delivery Catering

Preparing and delivering meals to individuals and families. This is growing rapidly with busy professionals who want home-cooked meals but don't have time to cook.

Specialty Catering

Focusing on specific cuisines: intercontinental, traditional Nigerian, vegan, or dietary-specific (gluten-free, diabetic-friendly). Specialty catering commands premium prices.

Get the Necessary Permits

  • CAC registration: Register your catering business.
  • Local government permit: Food business permit from your local government.
  • NAFDAC registration: If you're packaging any food products.
  • Health certificate: You and your staff should have medical fitness certificates.
  • Fire safety certificate: For your kitchen premises.

Set Up Your Kitchen

You need a commercial-grade kitchen. This can be a dedicated space or your home kitchen (if it meets commercial standards and local regulations allow it).

Essential equipment:

  • Industrial cookers and ovens (₦200,000 - ₦800,000)
  • Large pots, pans, and cooking utensils (₦100,000 - ₦300,000)
  • Refrigerators and freezers (₦200,000 - ₦600,000)
  • Food warmers and chafing dishes for events (₦100,000 - ₦400,000)
  • Serving plates, trays, and cutlery (₦100,000 - ₦300,000)
  • Food storage containers and packaging (₦50,000 - ₦150,000)
  • Generator or inverter for power backup (₦300,000 - ₦1 million)

Plan Your Menu

Your menu should reflect:

  • What you do best: Don't offer 50 dishes. Offer 10-15 dishes that you cook exceptionally well.
  • Local preferences: In Nigeria, jollof rice, fried rice, pounded yam, egusi soup, and grilled meats are always in demand.
  • Event type: Wedding menus differ from corporate lunch menus. Have different packages for different events.
  • Dietary requirements: Include options for vegetarians, people with allergies, and specific cultural or religious dietary needs.

Pricing Your Services

Catering in Nigeria is typically priced per head (per person). Current rates range from:

  • Basic catering: ₦3,000 - ₦8,000 per person
  • Mid-range catering: ₦8,000 - ₦20,000 per person
  • Premium catering: ₦20,000 - ₦50,000+ per understanding registering your catering businessthe food industryperson

Your pricing should cover food costs (typically 35-40% of the price), labor, transportation, equipment rental, and your profit margin.

Build Your Team

A catering business needs:

  • Head chef: The person who creates recipes and oversees cooking quality
  • Cooks: Depending on the scale of events you handle
  • Kitchen assistants: For food preparation, cleaning, and packing
  • Delivery/pickup staff: For transporting food to finding reliable food suppliersevent venues
  • Event coordinators: For on-site setup and service at events

For small events, you might handle everything yourself with 2-3 assistants. For large weddings (500+ guests), you'll need a team of 10-20 people.

Get Your First Clients

  • Start with your network: Tell everyone you know about your catering service
  • Offer tasting sessions: Let potential clients taste your food before booking
  • Partner with event planners: They're always looking for reliable caterers to recommend
  • Social media: Post mouth-watering photos of your dishes on Instagram
  • Business directories: List on BusinessMen so event organizers can find you

Startup Cost Estimate

For a small to medium catering business:

  • Business registration and permits: ₦100,000 - ₦300,000
  • Kitchen equipment: ₦1 million - ₦3 million
  • Serving equipment and packaging: ₦300,000 - ₦800,000
  • Initial food supplies: ₦200,000 - ₦500,000
  • Marketing and branding: ₦100,000 - ₦300,000
  • Working capital: ₦500,000 - ₦1 million
  • Total: ₦2.2 million - ₦5.9 million

Final Thoughts

The catering business in Nigeria is competitive but always in demand. Success comes from consistent food quality, reliable service, and good customer relationships. Every event you cater is a walking advertisement. Make the food exceptional, the presentation beautiful, and the service professional, and your clients will keep coming back and referring others.
