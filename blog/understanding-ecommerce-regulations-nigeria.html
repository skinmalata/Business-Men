Understanding E-commerce Regulations for Nigerian Sellers

By BusinessMen Editorial 7 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Selling online in Nigeria has never been easier. With platforms like Instagram, WhatsApp, Jumia, Konga, and your own website, you can reach customers across the country without a physical shop. But running an e-commerce business isn't just about posting products and collecting payments. There are legal requirements you need to follow.

Business Registration

Even if you're selling from your phone on Instagram, you should register your business with the CAC. This gives you legal standing, allows you to open a corporate bank account, and builds trust with customers.

For small online sellers, a business name registration (₦20,000-₦30,000) is sufficient. registering building your online storeyour e-commerce businessIf you plan to scale, register as a limited liability company.

Tax Obligations

Online businesses are subject to the same tax laws as physical businesses:

  • Company Income Tax: If you're registered as a company, you pay CIT on your profits. Small companies (turnunder ₦25 million) pay 0%.
  • Value Added Tax: VAT at 7.5% applies to most goods and services sold online.
  • Personal Income Tax: If you're a sole proprietor, you pay personal income tax on your business profits.

The FIRS has been increasingly focused on taxing digital businesses. Don't assume that because you're online, you're invisible to tax authorities.

Consumer Protection

The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC) regulates e-commerce in Nigeria. Key requirements include:

  • Clear product descriptions: Your product descriptions must be accurate. Don't mislead customers about what they're buying.
  • Transparent pricing: Display the full price including any additional charges (delivery, taxes).
  • Return and refund policy: You must have a clear policy for returns, exchanges, and refunds.
  • Privacy policy: If you collect customer data (names, addresses, phone numbers), you must have a privacy policy explaining how you use and protect that data.
  • Terms and conditions: Your website or store should have clear terms of service.

NDPR Compliance

The Nigeria Data Protection Regulation (NDPR) requires businesses that collect personal data to:

  • Obtain consent before collecting personal data
  • Protect customer data from unauthorized access
  • Allow customers to access, correct, or delete their data
  • Report data breaches within 72 hours
  • File annual data protection audits (for businesses processing data of more than 2,000 individuals)

If your e-commerce business collects customer names, addresses, phone numbers, or payment information, you're subject to NDPR.

Payment Regulations

If you accept online payments, you need to comply with Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) regulations:

  • Use licensed payment processors: Paystack, Flutterwave, Interswitch, and other CBN-licensed payment gateways are safe choices.
  • PCI DSS compliance: If you store or process card data, you must comply with the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard.
  • Anti-money laundering: Report suspicious transactions and maintain records of all financial transactions.

Product-Specific Regulations

Depending on what you sell, additional regulations may apply:

  • Food and beverages: NAFDAC registration is required for packaged food products.
  • Drugs and supplements: NAFDAC approval and PCN oversight for pharmaceutical products.
  • Cosmetics: NAFDAC registration for manufactured cosmetics.
  • Electronics: SON (Standards Organisation of Nigeria) certification for electrical and electronic products.
  • Imported goods: Customs clearance, import duties, and relevant product certifications.

Intellectual Property

Protect your brand and respect others' intellectual property:

  • Trademark your brand name and logo: This prevents others from using your brand.
  • Don't sell counterfeit products: Selling fake or counterfeit goods is illegal and can result in criminal prosecution.
  • Respect copyright: Don't use other people's product photos, descriptions, or branding without permission.

Best Practices for Nigerian E-commerce Sellers

  • Have a physical address: Even if you're online-only, having a verifiable address builds trust.
  • Use a corporate bank account: Don't receive business payments into a personal account.
  • Provide clear contact information: Phone number, email, and WhatsApp number for customer inquiries.
  • Be transparent about delivery times: Don't promise next-day delivery if it takes 3 days.
  • Keep records: Maintain records of all transactions, customer communications, and supplier invoices.
  • Get insurance: Consider goods-in-transit insurance for deliveries.

Final Thoughts

The regulatory environment for e-commerce in Nigeria is evolving. Stay informed about changes in laws and regulations. When in doubt, consult a lawyer who specializes in e-commerce and digital business law.

Compliance isn't just about avoiding penalties. It's about building a legitimate, trustworthy business that customers feel confident buying from. And in the competitive world of online retail, trust is your most valuable asset.
