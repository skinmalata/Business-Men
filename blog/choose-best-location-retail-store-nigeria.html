How to Choose the Best Location for Your Retail Store

By BusinessMen Editorial 7 min read Updated May 20, 2026

The three most important words in retail are location, location, location. You've probably heard it a hundred times, but it's true. A great product in a bad location will struggle. A decent product in a great location can thrive.

Choosing the right location for your retail store in Nigeria requires careful analysis. The rent you pay is understanding finding a good real estate agentcommercial real estateone of your biggest fixed costs, and getting it wrong can sink your business before it even starts.

Know Your Target Customer

Before you look at any location, define exactly who your customer is. A luxury boutique targeting high-income professionals needs a completely different location from a budget phone accessories shop targeting students.

Ask yourself:

  • What is my customer's income level?
  • Where do they live?
  • Where do they work?
  • Where do they shop already?
  • How do they commute?

If you're selling premium skincare products, marketing your new retail storeyour customers are likely in areas like Lekki, Ikoyi, Maitama, or GRA Port Harcourt. If you're selling affordable phone accessories, you want to be near universities, motor parks, or busy market areas.

Foot Traffic Analysis

Foot traffic is the number of people walking past your store. More foot traffic generally means more potential customers. But not all foot traffic is equal.

Spend time at your potential location at different times of day and different days of the week. Count how many people walk by. More importantly, count how many look like your target customer.

A location on a busy highway might have thousands of cars passing by, but if nobody stops, that traffic is useless. You want a location where people actually walk, browse, and shop.

Visibility and Accessibility

Your store should be easy to see and easy to reach. Consider:

  • Street visibility: Can people see your shop from the road? Is your sign visible?
  • Parking: Is there parking space for customers who drive?
  • Public transport: Is it accessible by bus, danfo, or keke?
  • Walkability: Is the area pedestrian-friendly?
  • Ground floor vs. upper floor: Ground floor locations almost always get more foot traffic than upper floors.

In Nigerian cities, a shop on the ground floor of a busy plaza is worth significantly more than one on the third floor, even if they're the same size. Don't sacrifice visibility for cheaper rent without careful consideration.

Competition and Complementary Businesses

Having competitors nearby isn't always bad. In fact, it can be good. A cluster of similar businesses creates a destination that attracts more customers than a single isolated shop.

Think about Computer Village in Ikeja. Hundreds of phone and computer shops packed together. You'd think the competition would be brutal, but the concentration of sellers draws customers from across the country. The pie is bigger because everyone is in one place.

Also look for complementary businesses. A women's fashion store does well near a salon or a shoe shop. A restaurant benefits from being near offices, cinemas, or shopping centers.

Cost vs. Revenue Potential

Here's the calculation you need to make: will the revenue from this location justify the rent?

If rent is ₦2 million per year, that's roughly ₦167,000 per month. If your profit margin is 30%, you need to generate at least ₦557,000 in monthly sales just to cover rent. Can this location deliver that volume?

Don't automatically go for the cheapest location. A cheap shop in a dead area will cost you more in lost sales than an expensive shop in a busy area costs in rent.

Infrastructure and Utilities

In Nigeria, infrastructure matters more than in many other countries. Check:

  • Power supply: How reliable is electricity in the area? What's the cost of running a generator?
  • Water supply: Is there running water?
  • Road condition: Can customers and delivery vehicles access the shop easily, especially during rainy season?
  • Security: Is the area safe? Are there incidents of theft or vandalism?
  • Internet connectivity: If you need internet for POS transactions or inventory management, is the network reliable?

Future Development Plans

Research any planned developments in the area. A new road, shopping mall, or residential estate could increase foot traffic and property values. Conversely, planned road construction could block access to your shop for months.

Check with the local government development authority about any planned projects in the area. This information can give you a competitive edge in choosing your location.

Negotiating the Lease

Commercial rent in Nigeria is typically paid annually or bi-annually. Here are some negotiation tips:

  • Ask for a rent-free period: Many landlords will give you 1-3 months rent-free for shop fitting and setup.
  • Negotiate rent increases: Try to cap annual rent increases at a specific percentage (10-15% is common in Nigeria).
  • Clarify what's included: Does the rent cover service charges, security, and waste disposal? Get it in writing.
  • Check the lease term: Longer leases give you stability but less flexibility. 2-3 years is a good starting point for a new business.

Final Thoughts

Choosing the right location is one of the most important decisions you'll make for your retail business. Take your time. Visit potential locations multiple times. Talk to other businesses in the area. Do the math on rent versus expected revenue.

The perfect location doesn't exist, but the right location for your specific business, budget, and target market absolutely does. Find it, negotiate well, and build your business around it.
