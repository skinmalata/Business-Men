The Complete Guide to Opening a School in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 12 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Education is one of the most recession-proof businesses in Nigeria. Parents will cut back on almost everything before they pull their children out of school. That's why the education sector continues to attract entrepreneurs, even in tough economic times.

But opening a school isn't like opening a shop. You're dealing with children, parents, government registering hiring teachers and staffyour school businessregulations, and the enormous responsibility of shaping young minds. Here's everything you need to know.

Decide on Your School Type

Nigeria has several categories of schools, each with different requirements:

Crèche and Nursery

For children aged 0-5 years. Requires child-friendly facilities, trained early childhood educators, and strict safety measures. The demand is high in urban areas where both parents work.

Primary School

For children aged 6-11 years (Primary 1-6). Follows the Nigerian primary education curriculum. Requires qualified primary school teachers, adequate classrooms, and approval from the state Ministry of Education.

Secondary School

For children aged 12-17 years (JSS 1-3 and SSS 1-3). Requires specialized subject teachers, science laboratories, a library, and sports facilities. Approval from the state Ministry of Education is mandatory.

International School

Follows international curricula like British (IGCSE), American, or IB. Commands higher fees but requires international-standard facilities, foreign-trained or experienced teachers, and accreditation from international bodies.

Legal Requirements and Registration

Step 1: Register Your Business with CAC

Register your school as a business name or limited liability company with the Corporate Affairs Commission. Most school owners opt for a limited company for credibility and liability protection.

Step 2: Get Approval from the Ministry of Education

This is the most critical step. Every school in Nigeria must be approved by the state Ministry of Education. The process involves:

  • Submitting a formal application letter
  • Providing details of the school's ownership, management, and staff qualifications
  • Submitting the proposed curriculum
  • Facility inspection by ministry officials
  • Meeting minimum standards choosing the right school locationfor classroom size, toilet facilities, playground, and safety

The ministry will issue a provisional approval, which becomes permanent after a satisfactory evaluation period (usually one academic year).

Step 3: Register with Other Relevant Bodies

  • WAEC/NECO: If you're running a secondary school, you need to register as an examination center.
  • TRCN: All teachers should be registered with the Teachers Registration Council of Nigeria.
  • Local government: You'll need local government permits and signage approvals.

Choosing a Location

Location is critical for a school. Consider:

  • Residential areas: Parents prefer schools close to home. Areas with young families are ideal.
  • Accessibility: Can school buses and parents' cars access the location easily?
  • Space: You need enough land for classrooms, a playground, parking, and future expansion.
  • Safety: The area should be safe, quiet, and free from industrial hazards.
  • Competition: Research existing schools in the area. Is there room for another? What gap can you fill?

In Lagos, areas like Lekki, Ikeja GRA, and Surulere are popular for schools. In Abuja, Maitama, Wuse, and Garki are prime locations. But don't ignore emerging residential areas where demand might be underserved.

Facility Requirements

Your school facility must meet minimum standards set by the Ministry of Education:

  • Classrooms: Well-ventilated, adequately lit, with appropriate furniture. Maximum of 25-30 pupils per classroom for primary schools.
  • Administrative offices: For the proprietor, headteacher, and administrative staff.
  • Toilets: Separate toilets for boys, girls, and staff. Clean and functional.
  • Playground: Safe, open space for physical education and recreation.
  • Library: Stocked with age-appropriate books and learning materials.
  • Science laboratory: Required for secondary schools, equipped with basic science equipment.
  • Computer lab: Increasingly expected even at primary level.
  • Sick bay: A basic first aid room with a trained nurse.
  • Security: Fenced perimeter, security guard, and controlled access.

Curriculum Planning

Your curriculum should align with the Nigerian Educational Research and Development Council (NERDC) standards. For primary and secondary schools, this means following the national curriculum.

Many schools add value by incorporating:

  • ICT and coding classes
  • French or other foreign languages
  • Music and arts programs
  • Entrepreneurship education
  • Religious and moral instruction
  • Extra-curricular activities (sports, debate, drama)

Staffing Your School

Your teachers are the heart of your school. Hire carefully:

  • Qualified teachers: Minimum of NCE (National Certificate in Education) for primary schools and a degree in education or relevant subject for secondary schools.
  • Experience: Mix experienced teachers with fresh graduates. Experienced teachers bring stability; fresh graduates bring energy and new ideas.
  • Support staff: You'll need administrative staff, cleaners, security guards, a driver (for school buses), and potentially a nurse.
  • Background checks: Every staff member who interacts with children should undergo thorough background checks.

Salary expectations vary by location and school type. In Lagos, a qualified primary school teacher might earn ₦80,000 to ₦200,000 monthly depending on experience and the school's fee structure.

Setting Your Fees

Research what other schools in your area charge. Your fees should reflect:

  • The quality of your facilities
  • Teacher qualifications and experience
  • Curriculum and extra-curricular offerings
  • Your target market's income level
  • Your operational costs

Don't underprice yourself. Parents often associate low fees with low quality. But don't overprice either. Your fees must be competitive within your market segment.

Marketing Your School

Getting your first students is the hardest part. Here's how to attract them:

  • Open days: Invite parents to tour your facility and meet your staff.
  • Community engagement: Sponsor local events, participate in community activities.
  • Online presence: A professional website, active social media, and a listing on BusinessMen.
  • Word of mouth: Offer referral discounts to parents who bring new students.
  • Launch promotions: Discounted fees for the first term or free registration for early enrollees.

Startup Costs

Opening a school in Nigeria requires significant capital. Here's a rough estimate for a small to medium primary school:

  • Property rent or purchase: ₦5 million - ₦50 million+
  • Renovation and furnishing: ₦3 million - ₦15 million
  • Educational materials and equipment: ₦1 million - ₦5 million
  • Registration and licensing: ₦500,000 - ₦2 million
  • Marketing and launch: ₦500,000 - ₦2 million
  • Working capital (6 months): ₦5 million - ₦15 million
  • Total estimated startup cost: ₦15 million - ₦90 million+

These are rough estimates. Your actual costs will depend on location, size, and the standard of school you're opening.

Final Thoughts

Opening a school is a long-term investment. You won't make massive profits in the first year, and probably not in the first three years. But a well-run school builds a reputation over time, and that reputation becomes your most valuable asset.

Focus on quality education, happy students, and satisfied parents. The rest will follow. And remember, you're not just running a business. You're shaping the future of the children who walk through your doors every morning.
