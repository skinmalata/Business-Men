How to Build a Professional Website for Your Business

By BusinessMen Editorial 8 min read Updated May 20, 2026

When a potential customer hears about your business, what's the first thing they do? They search for you online. If they find a professional website, they're more likely to trust you and do business with you. If they find nothing, or worse, a poorly designed website, they'll move on to your competitor.

Building a professional website for your Nigerian business doesn't have to be combining your website with directory listingsexpensive or complicated. Here's a step-by-step guide to getting it done right.

Step 1: Choose Your Website Platform

Website Builders (Easiest)

Platforms like Wix, Squarespace, and WordPress.com let you build a website without any coding knowledge. You choose a template, customize it with your content, and publish. These are perfect for small businesses that need a simple, professional website quickly.

Cost: ₦5,000 to ₦30,000 per month.

WordPress (Most Popular)

WordPress powers over 40% of all websites. It's free, flexible, and has thousands of themes and plugins. You'll need to buy hosting and a domain name, but the platform itself is free.

Cost: ₦10,000 to ₦50,000 per year for hosting and domain.

Custom Development (Most Flexible)

If you need a complex website with custom features, hiring a web developer is the way to go. This is more expensive but gives you complete control over design and functionality.

Cost: ₦200,000 to ₦2 million+ depending on complexity.

Step 2: Get a Domain Name

Your domain name is your website's address (e.g., yourbusiness.com.ng). Choose something that's:

  • Short and memorable: Easy to type and remember
  • Related to your business: Ideally your business name
  • Easy to spell: Avoid hyphens, numbers, and unusual spellings

For Nigerian businesses, consider both .com and .com.ng domains. The .com.ng domain signals that you're a Nigerian business, which can build trust with local customers.

Domain registration costs about ₦3,000 to ₦10,000 per year through registrars like Whogohost, QServers, or DomainKing.

Step 3: Choose a Hosting Provider

Web hosting is where your website files live. For Nigerian businesses, consider:

  • Local hosting: Nigerian hosting providers like Whogohost, QServers, and HostNowNow offer servers in Nigeria, which can mean faster loading speeds for Nigerian visitors.
  • International hosting: Providers like Bluehost, SiteGround, and Hostinger offer reliable hosting with global reach.

For a small business website, shared hosting at ₦5,000 to ₦20,000 per year is sufficient. As your traffic grows, you can upgrade to VPS or dedicated hosting.

Step 4: Design Your Website

Your website should include these essential pages:

Home Page

The first page visitors see. It should clearly state what your business does, who you serve, and what makes you different. Include a clear call-to-action (e.g., "Call Us Now," "Get a Quote," "Shop Now").

About Page

Tell your story. Who are you? Why did you start this business? What are your values? Nigerian customers appreciate businesses with a personal touch. Share your story and build trust.

Services/Products Page

Describe what you offer in detail. Include prices if possible, or at least a starting price. Use high-quality photos of your products or examples of your services.

Contact Page

Make it easy for customers to reach you. Include your phone number, email address, physical address, WhatsApp number, and a contact form. Add a Google Maps embed so customers can find your location.

Testimonials/Reviews Page

Showcase customer reviews and testimonials. Social proof is powerful in Nigeria, where trust is a major factor in purchasing decisions.

Step 5: Optimize for Mobile

Over 80% of Nigerians access the internet through their phones. Your website must look good and work well on mobile devices. Most modern website templates are mobile-responsive by default, but always test your website on a phone before launching.

Step 6: Add Essential Features

  • WhatsApp chat button: Nigerians love WhatsApp. Add a WhatsApp chat button so visitors can message you directly.
  • Google Analytics: Track how many people visit your website, where they come from, and what they do.
  • SEO optimization: Use relevant keywords in your page titles, descriptions, and content so Google can find your website.
  • Social media links: Connect your website to your social media profiles.
  • Fast loading speed: Optimize images and use a reliable hosting provider. Slow websites lose visitors.
Building your online presence? While you work on your website, make sure you're also listed on BusinessMen so customers can find you through multiple online channels.

Step 7: Launch and Maintain

Before launching, test everything: links, contact forms, mobile responsiveness, and loading speed. Ask a few people to review your website and give feedback.

After launching, maintain your website by:

  • Updating content regularly
  • Adding new products or services
  • Posting blog articles to improve SEO
  • Backing up your website regularly
  • Updating software and plugins for security

How Much Does a Business Website Cost in Nigeria?

  • DIY with website builder: ₦60,000 - ₦360,000 per year
  • WordPress with freelance developer: ₦150,000 - ₦500,000 (one-time) + ₦20,000 - ₦50,000 per year for hosting
  • Custom development: ₦300,000 - ₦2 million+ (one-time) + ₦30,000 - ₦100,000 per year for hosting

Final Thoughts

A professional website is one of the best investments you can make for your business. It works for you 24/7, reaching customers even when you're asleep. It builds credibility, showcases your products and services, and provides a platform for customers to contact you.

Don't let perfectionism stop you from launching. Start with a simple, professional website and improve it over time. The most important thing is to get started.
