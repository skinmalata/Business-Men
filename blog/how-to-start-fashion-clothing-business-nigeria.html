How to Start a Fashion and Clothing Business in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 8 min read Updated May 20, 2026

Nigeria's fashion industry is one of the most vibrant in Africa. From traditional Ankara designs to contemporary streetwear, Nigerian fashion entrepreneurs are building brands that compete globally. If you have an eye for style and a passion for fashion, this could be your business.

Choose Your Fashion Niche

The fashion industry is vast. Narrow your focus:

Ready-to-Wear Clothing

Pre-made clothing in standard sizes. This includes casual wear, corporate wear, and traditional attire. Ready-to-wear has a broad market but faces stiff competition from imported clothing.

Custom Tailoring

Made-to-measure clothing. Nigerian customers love custom-fitted outfits, especially for special occasions. This requires skilled tailors and good customer relationships.

Fashion Brand/Label

Creating your own branded clothing line. This involves designing, manufacturing (or outsourcing), and marketing under your brand name. Higher margins but requires strong branding.

Import and Retail

Importing clothing from China, Turkey, or other countries and selling in Nigeria. This requires knowledge of international sourcing, customs procedures, and local market preferences.

Accessories

Shoes, bags, jewelry, hats, and other fashion accessories. Lower capital requirement than full clothing lines and growing demand in Nigeria.

Source Your Materials or Products

For Local Production

Fabric markets in Nigeria are abundant. Key sourcing locations:

  • Kano: Kantin Kwari market for textiles
  • Lagos: Balogun market, Trade Fair Complex
  • Onitsha: Main market for wholesale fabrics
  • Aba: Ariaria market for materials and registering your fashion brandfinished goods

For premium fabrics, you can import from Turkey, China, or India. Turkish fabrics are particularly popular for Nigerian fashion because of their quality.

For Import and Retail

Popular sourcing destinations include:

  • China: Guangzhou for affordable clothing in bulk
  • Turkey: Istanbul for quality mid-range fashion
  • India: Surat and Mumbai for fabrics and garments
  • Italy: For premium luxury fashion

Factor in shipping costs, customs duties, and clearing agent fees when calculating your landed cost.

Set Up Your Production or Store

For Production-Based Business

  • Workshop space: A well-lit, ventilated space with enough room for cutting tables, sewing machines, and finishing areas
  • Equipment: Industrial sewing machines, overlock machines, cutting tools, pressing equipment
  • Skilled tailors: Hire experienced tailors who can execute your designs accurately
  • Pattern makers: For creating consistent sizing across your collections

For Retail-Based Business

  • Shop location: High-traffic areas with your target demographic
  • Interior design: Attractive displays, good lighting, fitting rooms
  • Inventory management: Track stock levels, sizes, and bestsellers

Build Your Brand

In fashion, your brand is everything. Invest in:

  • A memorable brand name and logo
  • Professional photography: Your product photos are your primary sales tool, especially online
  • Packaging: Branded bags, tags, and packaging elevate your brand perception
  • Consistent aesthetic: Your Instagram feed, website, and physical store should all reflect the same brand identity

Pricing Your Products

Calculate your costs carefully:

  • Materials/fabric cost
  • Labor cost
  • Overhead (rent, utilities, equipment depreciation)
  • Marketing and packaging
  • Desired profit margin (typically 50-100% markup on cost)

Don't underprice your products. Nigerian fashion consumers are willing to pay for quality and brand value. Price yourself too low, and people will assume your quality is low too.

Market Your Fashion Business

Fashion marketing in Nigeria is heavily visual and social:

  • Instagram: Your primary marketing platform. Post daily, use relevant hashtags, collaborate with influencers
  • Influencer marketing: Send free products to fashion influencers in exchange for posts
  • Fashion shows and exhibitions: Participate in Lagos Fashion Week, Arise Fashion Week, and local fashion events
  • WhatsApp marketing: Send new collection photos to your customer database
  • Pop-up shops: Temporary shops in malls and event venues to reach new customers

Startup Cost Estimate

For a small fashion brand/retail store:

  • Business registration: ₦30,000 - ₦50,000
  • Shop rent (1 year): ₦500,000 - ₦3 million
  • Interior and displays: ₦300,000 - ₦1 million
  • Initial inventory: ₦500,000 - ₦3 million
  • Equipment (if producing): ₦500,000 - ₦2 million
  • Branding and photography: ₦200,000 - ₦500,000
  • Marketing: ₦200,000 - ₦500,000
  • Working capital: ₦500,000 - ₦1 million
  • Total: ₦2.7 million - ₦11 million

Final Thoughts

The fashion business in Nigeria is competitive but rewarding. Success comes from unique designs, quality production, strong branding, and consistent marketing. Stay on top of trends, listen to your customers, and never compromise on quality.

Nigerian fashion is gaining global recognition. Position your brand well, and you could be part of the next wave of African fashion success stories.
