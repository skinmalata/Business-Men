How to Start a Cleaning Services Business in Nigeria

By BusinessMen Editorial 7 min read Updated May 20, 2026

As more Nigerians live busy lives and more businesses occupy office spaces, the demand for professional cleaning services keeps growing. From residential deep cleaning to corporate office maintenance, post-construction cleanup to fumigation, the cleaning industry offers multiple revenue streams for entrepreneurs.

The best part? You can start with relatively low capital and registering marketing your cleaning servicesyour cleaning businessscale as you build your client base. Let me show you how.

Choose Your Cleaning Niche

Don't try to do everything at once. Pick a niche to start with:

Residential Cleaning

Regular home cleaning, deep cleaning, move-in/move-out cleaning. Target busy professionals, dual-income families, and expatriates in areas like Lekki, Ikoyi, Maitama, and GRA Port Harcourt. Charge ₦15,000 to ₦50,000 per session depending on the size of the property and services required.

Commercial/Office Cleaning

Daily or weekly cleaning of offices, banks, and commercial buildings. These are contract-based and provide steady, predictable income. A small office cleaning contract might pay ₦100,000 to ₦300,000 monthly.

Post-Construction Cleaning

Cleaning buildings after construction or renovation. This is intensive work but pays well. A post-construction clean for a 4-bedroom house can cost ₦100,000 to ₦300,000.

Carpet and Upholstery Cleaning

Specialized cleaning using steam cleaning equipment. Hotels, offices, and wealthy homeowners are your target market.

Fumigation and Pest Control

Requires additional training and chemicals but is highly profitable. Every building needs periodic fumigation.

Register Your Business

Register with the CAC. A business name registration is sufficient to start, but a limited company gives more credibility for corporate contracts.

You may also need:

  • Local government permit: For operating a service business in your area.
  • NAFDAC approval: If you're manufacturing or importing cleaning chemicals.
  • Environmental permits: For waste disposal and chemical handling.

Get the Right Equipment

Your equipment is your toolkit. Start with the essentials:

  • Vacuum cleaner (₦50,000 - ₦200,000)
  • Pressure washer (₦80,000 - ₦300,000)
  • Floor polisher/scrubber (₦100,000 - ₦400,000)
  • Steam cleaner for carpets (₦80,000 - ₦250,000)
  • Cleaning chemicals and solutions (₦30,000 - ₦80,000 initial stock)
  • Mops, brooms, buckets, microfiber cloths (₦20,000 - ₦50,000)
  • Protective gear: gloves, masks, boots (₦15,000 - ₦30,000)
  • Ladder and extension poles (₦20,000 - ₦50,000)

You don't need to buy everything at once. Start with basic equipment and add specialized tools as your service offerings expand.

Hire and Train Staff

Your cleaners are your brand. Hire carefully:

  • Conduct background checks and require guarantors
  • Train them on cleaning techniques, chemical handling, and customer service
  • Provide uniforms with your company logo for professionalism
  • Pay fairly and on time. Cleaners are often underpaid, and fair compensation reduces turnover
  • Supervise their work, especially in the beginning

Start with 2-3 cleaners and scale as you get more contracts. For residential cleaning, you might need 2 people per job. For commercial cleaning, you might need a team of 4-6 depending on the size of the building.

Set Your Prices

Research what other cleaning companies in your area charge. Price based on:

  • Size of the property (number of rooms, square meters)
  • Type of cleaning (regular, deep, post-construction)
  • Frequency (one-time, weekly, monthly contract)
  • Additional services (carpet cleaning, window cleaning, fumigation)

Don't underprice yourself. Cheap prices attract difficult clients and make it hard to pay your staff fairly. Position yourself as a quality service provider and price accordingly.

Get Your First Clients

Start with your network. Tell friends, family, and colleagues about your business. Offer discounted rates for your first few clients in exchange for testimonials and referrals.

For corporate clients:

  • Visit office buildings and introduce yourself to facility managers
  • Send professional proposals to companies in your target area
  • Offer a free trial clean to demonstrate your quality
  • Network at business events and chamber of commerce meetings

Online marketing:

  • Create an Instagram page showing before-and-after photos of your work
  • List your business on BusinessMen and other directories
  • Run targeted Facebook ads in your service area
  • Encourage satisfied clients to leave reviews

Startup Cost Estimate

For a small cleaning services business in Lagos:

  • Business registration: ₦30,000 - ₦50,000
  • Basic equipment: ₦300,000 - ₦800,000
  • Cleaning supplies (initial stock): ₦50,000 - ₦100,000
  • Uniforms and branding: ₦50,000 - ₦100,000
  • Marketing: ₦50,000 - ₦150,000
  • Working capital (2 months): ₦200,000 - ₦500,000
  • Total: ₦680,000 - ₦1.7 million

Scaling Your Business

Once you have steady clients, consider:

  • Adding more service offerings (fumigation, carpet cleaning, exterior cleaning)
  • Expanding to neighboring cities
  • Hiring a supervisor to manage teams while you focus on business development
  • Investing in better equipment to improve efficiency
  • Building a website with online booking

Final Thoughts

The cleaning services business in Nigeria is growing, and there's room for professional, reliable operators. The key is consistency. Show up on time, deliver quality work, treat your clients' properties with respect, and they'll keep coming back.

Start small, do excellent work, and let your reputation grow your business. In the cleaning industry, word of mouth is your most powerful marketing tool.
